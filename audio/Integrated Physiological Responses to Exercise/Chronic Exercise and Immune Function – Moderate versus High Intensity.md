## Chronic Exercise and Immune Function – Moderate versus High Intensity

Repeated training makes the immune system and the neuroendocrine response adapt, and the result depends on intensity and recovery.
Moderate training with adequate recovery lowers baseline inflammation and improves immune regulation.
Chronic high-intensity training, on the other hand, suppresses several immune functions and raises inflammatory cytokines.
Athletes are not immunocompromised, as they typically suffer only upper respiratory tract infections.
Even so, training before full recovery may lead to chronic immunosuppression.

## 1. From repeated bouts to adaptation

Chronic exercise means training repeated over months and years, not a single acute bout.
Each bout mobilises immune cells into the blood and redistributes them to the tissues.
Repeating it leads to adaptations in the immune system and in the neuroendocrine, or stress-hormone, response.
This happens just as repeated training adapts muscle and heart.

## 2. Chronic moderate exercise with adequate recovery

At moderate intensity with adequate recovery, the adaptation leaves the number of blood leukocytes, or white blood cells, unchanged.
So the benefit lies in how immune cells work.
Immune cell trafficking becomes more efficient, meaning cells move between blood and tissues more readily.
Innate and adaptive regulation improves as well.
Part of the better regulation comes from increased T cells, including regulatory T cells, which suppress immune responses.
More brakes keep responses from overshooting.
Cytokines, the immune system's signalling proteins, are rebalanced too.
Baseline pro-inflammatory cytokines decrease and anti-inflammatory cytokines increase.
This reduces chronic low-grade inflammation, a mild, persistent, body-wide inflammation.
The result is lower baseline, or resting, inflammation, seen as a lower level of C-reactive protein, the liver protein that marks inflammation.
Training also brings a slower age-related immune decline, that is, less immunosenescence, the gradual weakening of immune function with age.
And salivary I G A, the antibody abundant in saliva, is maintained or increased.
A sample question asks which option is not associated with such training.
The odd one out is higher baseline pro-inflammatory cytokines, because the opposite is true.

## 3. Chronic high-intensity exercise

Chronic high-intensity training reverses much of this.
In a single bout, cortisol and the catecholamines, which are adrenaline and noradrenaline, mobilise immune cells.
But they become suppressive when hard training keeps them always switched on.
This chronic exposure to catecholamines and cortisol lowers the activity of natural killer, or N K, cells, the innate killers of virus-infected cells.
Neutrophils, the main phagocytes, show impaired phagocytosis and oxidative burst.
The oxidative burst is the burst of toxic reactive oxygen species that a neutrophil produces to kill what it has swallowed.
Monocytes and macrophages develop desensitised signalling, meaning their receptors stop responding to signals they see all the time.
Meanwhile, persistent muscle and tissue stress keeps interleukin six and tumour necrosis factor alpha raised.
Under high intensity, this interleukin six tends to be pro-inflammatory and can add to inflammation, because it uses a different signalling mechanism.

## 4. Are athletes immunocompromised?

So are athletes immunocompromised, with a seriously weakened immune defence?
Not in the usual sense, because athletes do not present with the serious illnesses normally associated with immunodeficiency.
They present only with upper respiratory tract infections.
However, repeated patterns of training before full recovery may eventually lead to chronic immunosuppression.
This is a lasting dampening of immunity, built from small changes in several parameters rather than one big defect.
It is therefore crucial to balance exercise intensity with rest, nutrition and recovery strategies.

## 5. The whole picture

These threads meet in the lecture's closing summary, a one-page answer to exercise immunology essays.
It reads along two axes, acute versus chronic, meaning one bout versus training, and moderate versus intense.
Acute moderate exercise typically enhances immune surveillance, which means patrolling for threats.
It brings increased N K cell activity, mobilisation of T cells, a transient rise in interleukin six and interleukin fifteen, and anti-inflammatory effects.
Here, the interleukin six is the anti-inflammatory kind.
Interleukin fifteen is another muscle-released cytokine, which supports N K and T cells.
Acute exercise that is very intense or prolonged can create the open window of temporary immune suppression, lasting three to seventy-two hours.
It brings lymphocytopenia, a fall in circulating lymphocytes, along with reduced N K cell cytotoxicity, or killing ability, and higher cortisol and catecholamines.
Both acute patterns rest on the hormones and cell movements of a single bout.
Chronic moderate exercise brings lower baseline inflammation and improved immune regulation.
Chronic, frequent intense exercise carries a risk of cumulative immunosuppression if recovery is insufficient.
That means persistent inflammation, elevated stress hormones, reduced antibody responses, and a higher susceptibility to infections, especially upper respiratory tract infections.
Read together, moderate exercise helps at both timescales.
Intense exercise, by contrast, suppresses immunity briefly after one bout, and cumulatively without recovery.

## Summary

To sum up, repeated mobilisation of immune cells makes the immune system and the neuroendocrine response adapt.
With moderate intensity and adequate recovery, leukocyte numbers are unchanged, but trafficking and regulation improve.
T cells, including regulatory T cells, increase.
Pro-inflammatory cytokines and C-reactive protein fall, while anti-inflammatory cytokines rise.
Immunosenescence slows, and salivary I G A is maintained.
With chronic high intensity, the function of N K cells, neutrophils, monocytes and macrophages falls.
Interleukin six and tumour necrosis factor alpha rise, causing low-grade systemic inflammation, because hormonal and tissue stress never switch off.
Athletes are not immunocompromised, suffering only upper respiratory tract infections, but training before full recovery can add up to chronic immunosuppression.
In short, acute moderate exercise enhances immune surveillance, and acute intense exercise opens a three to seventy-two hour window of suppression.
Chronic moderate exercise improves regulation, while chronic intense exercise without recovery risks cumulative immunosuppression.
And that's the long view of exercise and immunity, from a single bout to years of training.
