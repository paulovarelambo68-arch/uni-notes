## Lung Disease and Exercise – ARDS, Asthma and COVID

The lungs can fail in different ways, and knowing how each failure works explains who struggles to breathe and why.
This note follows three of those failures.
They are lungs that flood with fluid, airways that narrow, and the lingering effects of COVID on fitness.

## 1. When the alveoli flood: ARDS

We begin with A R D S, short for acute respiratory distress syndrome.
It is a sudden lung failure in which the alveoli fill with fluid.
The alveoli are the air sacs where oxygen enters the blood.
The commonest trigger of A R D S is pneumonia.
Pneumonia is caused mostly by bacteria such as Streptococcus pneumoniae, or by viruses such as influenza.
Rarely, it is caused by fungi or parasites.
The infection sets off a surge of neutrophils and cytokines, the immune cells and chemical signals of inflammation.
Together, they injure the alveolar-capillary membrane, the thin barrier between air and blood.
Fluid then leaks from the capillaries into the air spaces, which is called pulmonary oedema.
As a result, air can no longer reach the blood in those flooded spaces.
Doctors diagnose A R D S with the Berlin consensus definition, which has three parts.
The first is an acute onset, within about a week of the trigger.
The second part compares the oxygen reaching the blood with the oxygen breathed in.
It does this with a ratio, the arterial oxygen pressure divided by the fraction of inspired oxygen.
The arterial oxygen pressure is the oxygen pressure in arterial blood.
The fraction of inspired oxygen is the fraction of inhaled air that is oxygen.
Room air has a fraction of inspired oxygen of zero point two one.
A healthy lung breathing room air reaches an arterial oxygen pressure of about one hundred millimetres of mercury.
Its ratio is therefore roughly one hundred divided by zero point two one, which is about four hundred and seventy-five millimetres of mercury.
In A R D S, the ratio is three hundred millimetres of mercury or less.
Blood passing flooded alveoli never meets the inhaled air, so even extra oxygen raises the arterial oxygen pressure far less than it should.
The third part is a chest X-ray showing white shadows in both lungs, called bilateral infiltrates, consistent with pulmonary oedema.
On an X-ray, air lets the rays through and shows black, while fluid blocks them and shows white.
For that reason, healthy lungs look dark.
In A R D S, both lungs look hazy white, like a sponge soaked with water.
The diagnosis is applied inconsistently, which makes it hard to know the prevalence of A R D S, meaning how common it is.
Estimates are about three to six per hundred thousand person-years in Europe.
Worldwide, estimates range from one point five to five point three.
Put simply, a few people in every hundred thousand develop it each year.
The number of new cases, known as the incidence, appears to be rising.
Yet the share of patients who die, known as the mortality, is slowly falling, for four reasons.
First, A R D S is diagnosed better.
Second, treatments such as antibiotics now target its cause.
Third, supportive care, which keeps the patient alive while the lung heals, is better and starts earlier.
Fourth, artificial ventilation is gentler.
In a flooded lung, only some alveoli are still open.
Large, forceful machine breaths over-stretch those open alveoli and injure the lung further.

## 2. When the airways narrow: asthma

Let's turn to asthma, which narrows the airways instead of flooding the alveoli.
It affects seven per cent of Irish adults and twenty-five per cent of Irish children.
Up to half of these people with asthma are not well controlled.
Many attacks are set off by allergens.
These include house dust mite, pollen and mould spores.
Animal dander, the skin flakes shed by pets, is another.
Exercise can itself tighten the airways, a reaction named exercise-induced bronchospasm.
It happens because fast breathing cools and dries the airway lining, especially in cold, dry air.
The narrowing itself happens in two ways.
In a normal airway, the smooth muscle around it is relaxed and the passage is wide open.
In an asthmatic airway, the wall is inflamed and thickened, so the passage is narrower even between attacks.
During an attack, the smooth muscle has also tightened, squeezing the passage to a slit that mucus can easily block.
Air is then trapped in the alveoli, because the airways narrow further as the lungs empty.
Narrowed airways slow the airflow, which lowers the ratio of the forced expiratory volume in one second to the forced vital capacity.
These two measures are abbreviated as F E V one and F V C.
Their ratio is the share of a full forced breath out that leaves in the first second.
A low ratio is the obstructive pattern.
Medicines target the muscle and the wall separately.
Relievers are short-acting beta-two agonists, such as salbutamol, which is sold as Ventolin.
They stimulate the adrenaline receptors on airway smooth muscle, the beta-two adrenergic receptors.
This stimulation relaxes the muscle, which lets the airway widen within minutes.
Stimulating the same receptors also widens some arterioles, the smallest arteries.
Preventers are inhaled corticosteroids, such as Becotide, Flixotide and Ciclesonide.
These steroid drugs calm the inflamed wall over weeks of daily use.
At the heart of this inflammation are faulty macrophages.
Macrophages are the immune cells that patrol the airways and swallow microbes and debris.
In asthma, they go wrong in five ways.
First, they swallow fewer microbes, and this reduced phagocytosis raises the risk of infection.
Second, they clear away fewer dying cells, showing reduced efferocytosis.
The dying cells left behind keep drawing more immune cells into the lung.
Third, they release more of the inflammatory cytokines.
These are tumour necrosis factor alpha, along with interleukin one beta, interleukin six and interleukin eight.
Already inflamed, the macrophages cannot respond to a real bacterial signal such as lipopolysaccharide, a molecule from the walls of bacteria.
Fourth, they make more interleukin ten messenger ribonucleic acid, or messenger R N A, which is the gene's working copy.
However, they make less interleukin ten protein.
As a consequence, the signal that switches inflammation off is missing.
Fifth, the M two form, the repair type, predominates.
Its repair work remodels and scars the airway wall, which is called fibrosis.

## 3. After COVID: what three studies found

Finally, three studies have measured how COVID nineteen affected exercise capacity.
Exercise capacity is usually judged by maximal oxygen uptake, or V O two max, the most oxygen the body can use each minute.
In twenty-one professional footballers in Cyprus, even mild or moderate illness left silent effects that lasted longer than expected.
One such effect was a lower V O two max.
A football match is played near the lactate threshold, the intensity at which lactate starts to build up.
A footballer's fitness therefore depends on that threshold as well as on V O two max.
In Basel, about half of the patients tested three months after severe COVID nineteen had a reduced exercise capacity.
Their V O two max was below eighty-two per cent of the predicted value, the one expected for their age, sex and size.
Yet only a minority were limited by their lungs.
Most were limited instead by deconditioning, the fitness lost through illness and inactivity, or by the heart and circulation.
In one hundred and sixty-five elite athletes in Hungary, a well-built training plan restored good fitness in most of them within three months.
The infection did not restrict their careers in the long term.
Beyond COVID, exercise itself shapes the risk of catching respiratory infections.
Moderate exercise lowers that risk, whereas very heavy exercise raises it.

## Summary

To recap, A R D S is a sudden flooding of the alveoli, called pulmonary oedema, most often after pneumonia.
The cause is damage to the alveolar-capillary membrane by neutrophils and cytokines.
The Berlin definition needs an acute onset and bilateral infiltrates on X-ray.
Its remaining requirement is that the ratio of arterial oxygen pressure to the fraction of inspired oxygen is three hundred millimetres of mercury or less.
Normally, that ratio is about four hundred and seventy-five millimetres of mercury.
A R D S strikes about three to six per hundred thousand person-years in Europe.
Mortality is falling with better diagnosis, cause-targeted treatment, earlier support and gentler ventilation.
Turning to asthma, it affects seven per cent of Irish adults and twenty-five per cent of Irish children.
Its narrowed airways give a low F E V one to F V C ratio.
Relievers, the short-acting beta-two agonists, relax airway muscle within minutes.
Preventers, the inhaled corticosteroids, calm inflammation over weeks.
Asthmatic macrophages show reduced phagocytosis and efferocytosis, and too little interleukin ten protein.
Their M two form predominates and remodels the airway.
After COVID, silent effects lingered in footballers.
Half of severely ill patients had a lower V O two max, mainly from deconditioning.
Most elite athletes recovered within three months.
And that's how flooded alveoli, narrowed airways and the after-effects of COVID each limit breathing and fitness.
