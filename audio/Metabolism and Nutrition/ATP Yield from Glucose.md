## ATP Yield from Glucose

When glucose is broken down, its energy is captured as A T P, the cell's energy currency, at several points along the way.
In this note, we'll add up that A T P for one glucose molecule.
We'll also see why books give different totals, and how much of the energy in glucose is kept rather than lost as heat.

## 1. What the three stages hand over

Glucose is broken down in three stages.
Each stage hands over two kinds of product to the final count.
One is A T P made directly.
It forms when an enzyme moves a phosphate group from a fuel molecule onto A D P, a process called substrate level phosphorylation.
The other kind is the hydrogen carriers, N A D H and F A D H two.
They take hydrogen and its electrons to the electron transport chain.
This chain is a set of proteins inside the mitochondrion, where most A T P is made.
Glycolysis comes first.
It splits glucose into two pyruvate and gives a net two A T P and two N A D H.
Converting the two pyruvate into two acetyl co A gives two more N A D H.
The Krebs cycle, also called the citric acid cycle or T C A cycle, then turns twice, once for each acetyl co A.
These two turns give two A T P, six N A D H and two F A D H two.
Altogether, one glucose provides four A T P by substrate level phosphorylation, ten N A D H and two F A D H two.

## 2. The balance sheet

The carriers become A T P only when they pass their hydrogen to the electron transport chain.
There, the electrons flow to oxygen, and their energy drives A T P synthesis.
This process is called oxidative phosphorylation.
On average, each N A D H yields about two point five A T P, and each F A D H two about one point five.
The F A D H two figure is lower because it hands its electrons to the chain further along.
Now let's draw up the balance sheet, source by source.
Substrate level phosphorylation in glycolysis gives a net two A T P.
The two N A D H from glycolysis give two times two point five, or five A T P.
The two N A D H from the pyruvate to acetyl co A step give another five A T P.
Substrate level phosphorylation in the Krebs cycle gives two A T P.
From the six N A D H of the Krebs cycle come fifteen A T P.
Finally, the two F A D H two give two times one point five, or three A T P.
The total is thirty-two A T P.
Taken together, the ten N A D H and two F A D H two make twenty-eight A T P in the electron transport chain.
The word net matters here.
The complete breakdown of glucose forms thirty-four A T P in all.
Glycolysis makes four, the Krebs cycle makes two, and the electron transport chain makes twenty-eight.
But glycolysis had to spend two A T P at the start, to prime glucose with phosphate.
The net yield in skeletal muscle is therefore thirty-two A T P per glucose.
Let's follow one glucose from start to finish.
Glucose is primed with two A T P and split into two pyruvate, which yields four A T P, or two net.
The two pyruvate become two acetyl co A, which feed the citric acid cycle.
Along the way, carbon leaves as carbon dioxide.
Two molecules of carbon dioxide leave at the pyruvate step, and four leave in the cycle.
Every N A D H and F A D H two carries its hydrogen to the electron transport cytochromes, where the process ends in A T P.
The carriers then return empty as N A D plus and F A D, like delivery vans going back for another load.

## 3. Why other totals exist

Other books print thirty-six or thirty-eight A T P.
They use older values of three A T P per N A D H and two A T P per F A D H two.
On that count, the ten N A D H give thirty A T P and the two F A D H two give four.
Adding the four from substrate level phosphorylation gives thirty-eight A T P.
That is a theoretical maximum still printed in many textbooks.
There is a complication with the two N A D H made by glycolysis in the cytosol.
They cannot enter the mitochondrion, so a shuttle must carry their hydrogen in.
In the heart and liver, the malate aspartate shuttle delivers that hydrogen as N A D H.
Each of those N A D H still counts as three A T P, and the total stays at thirty-eight.
In skeletal muscle and the brain, the glycerol phosphate shuttle delivers that hydrogen on F A D.
Here each of those N A D H counts as only two A T P.
That gives thirty-six A T P, the older total for skeletal muscle.
The value of thirty-two is based on the measured averages of two point five and one point five, which makes it the one to use.

## 4. With oxygen and without

Without enough oxygen, glucose stops at pyruvate.
Pyruvate becomes lactate, which lets glycolysis keep running.
The yield in that case is only two A T P per glucose.
Fully oxidised glucose gives thirty-two A T P, which is sixteen times as much.
Rapid glycolysis on its own therefore delivers about six per cent of the A T P that glucose can give.
That share is often quoted as five per cent, because it was worked out from the older total of thirty-eight.
This means that a muscle short of oxygen must break down glucose about sixteen times faster to make the same A T P.
That is why all-out efforts drain muscle glycogen, the muscle's store of glucose, so quickly, and why such efforts cannot last.

## 5. Efficiency

Efficiency asks how much of the energy in glucose ends up in A T P.
Each mole of A T P formed from A D P and inorganic phosphate, known as P I, conserves about seven kilocalories, or seven point three kilocalories more precisely.
One mole of glucose holds six hundred and eighty-six kilocalories.
Thirty-two moles of A T P hold thirty-two times seven point three kilocalories, which is about two hundred and thirty-four kilocalories.
Dividing two hundred and thirty-four by six hundred and eighty-six gives a relative efficiency of about thirty-four per cent.
The other sixty-six per cent is released as heat.
This is the second law of thermodynamics at work, since every energy transfer loses some energy as heat.
The same heat explains why the body warms up during exercise.
The older counts give other numbers.
Thirty-six A T P conserve two hundred and sixty-three kilocalories, and thirty-eight A T P give an efficiency of about forty per cent.
Even thirty-four per cent is good for a machine.
A steam engine, by comparison, turns only roughly thirty per cent of its fuel into useful work.

## Summary

One glucose hands over four A T P made directly, ten N A D H and two F A D H two.
These come from glycolysis, the pyruvate to acetyl co A step and two turns of the Krebs cycle.
At two point five A T P per N A D H and one point five per F A D H two, the carriers give twenty-eight A T P.
Those twenty-eight A T P are made in the electron transport chain.
The process forms thirty-four A T P in all, but glycolysis spent two at the start.
The net yield is thirty-two A T P, with four from substrate level phosphorylation and twenty-eight from oxidative phosphorylation.
Totals of thirty-six and thirty-eight A T P come from the older values of three A T P per N A D H and two per F A D H two.
Without oxygen, glucose gives only two A T P, one sixteenth of the aerobic yield, which is why glycogen is used up quickly.
The thirty-two A T P hold about two hundred and thirty-four of the six hundred and eighty-six kilocalories in glucose, an efficiency of about thirty-four per cent.
The rest is released as heat.
