## Ascending Tracts

Ascending tracts are the pathways that carry somatosensory information from the receptors of the body up to the brain.
That information travels through the spinal cord and brainstem to the thalamus, and from there on to the somatosensory cortex.
Every one of these systems is organised as a chain of three neurons.
A first order neuron runs from the receptor.
A second order neuron crosses the midline and reaches the thalamus.
And a third order neuron projects from the thalamus to the cortex.
Two systems carry the exam weight, namely the dorsal column medial lemniscal system and the anterolateral, or spinothalamic, system.
We'll follow each of them in turn, then meet some smaller tracts, and end with where the thalamus fits in.

## Dorsal Column Medial Lemniscal System

The first of the two systems carries tactile sensation and proprioception, which make up the discriminative side of somatic sensation.
Its first order neurons are mainly A-beta fibres.
These are the large, fast, myelinated afferents for touch and pressure.
Their central processes ascend the spinal cord without crossing.
They terminate in the dorsal column nuclei in the medulla, and this is the first synapse of the pathway.
The second order neurons have their cell bodies in these nuclei.
Their axons cross the midline in the medulla and then project to the thalamus.
Because of this crossing, each cerebral hemisphere receives touch and proprioceptive information from the opposite side of the body.
Finally, the third order neurons project from the thalamus to the somatosensory cortex.
It is there that the conscious perception of touch and body position is generated.

## Anterolateral System (Spinothalamic)

Now for the anterolateral system, which carries temperature and pain, the protective side of somatic sensation.
Here the first order neurons are A-delta and C fibres, which are thin and slow compared with A-beta.
A-delta fibres carry fast, sharp pain and cold.
C fibres, on the other hand, carry slow, dull pain and warmth.
Unlike the dorsal column fibres, these terminate in the spinal cord itself, synapsing shortly after they enter it.
The second order neurons cross the midline within the spinal cord.
After crossing, they ascend and terminate in the thalamus, specifically in its ventral posterior complex, or V P.
As in the dorsal column system, the third order neurons project from the thalamus to the somatosensory cortex.
That brings us to the key contrast.
The dorsal column system crosses in the medulla, whereas the anterolateral system crosses in the spinal cord.
In other words, the two share the same three neuron plan but differ in crossing point, fibre type and modality.

## Other Ascending Tracts

Beyond these two conscious pathways, the spinal cord gives rise to smaller tracts, each named after its target.
Their job is adjustment and coordination, not perception.
First, the spinoreticular tract ascends together with the anterolateral system to the reticular formation of the brainstem.
It carries the arousal and affective side of pain.
That means the unpleasantness and autonomic activation that accompany a noxious stimulus, rather than its location or quality.
Next, the spinocerebellar tracts carry proprioceptive information from muscle spindle and related afferents directly to the cerebellum.
Afferents from the lower body relay in Clarke's nucleus.
This is the input the cerebellum uses to coordinate movement, and it never reaches consciousness.
Last comes the spino olivary tract, which projects from the cord to the inferior olive.
The climbing fibres of the inferior olive supply the cerebellum with a training signal that adjusts movement circuits.

## The Thalamus as the Sensory Relay

Stepping back, both systems fit into a wider plan of sensory projection.
Most sensory pathways project to the thalamus, which modifies the information and relays it to the cortical centres.
There are two exceptions.
One is olfaction, which projects from the nose through the olfactory bulb directly to the olfactory cortex.
The other is the equilibrium pathways, which project to the cerebellum.

## Summary

Pulling the threads together, ascending tracts are three neuron chains that carry somatosensory information from the body to the cortex.
The thalamus is the relay that modifies and forwards nearly all sensory input.
The exceptions are olfaction, which goes straight to its own cortex, and equilibrium, which goes to the cerebellum.
The two exam systems split the work by modality.
Touch and proprioception take the dorsal column medial lemniscal route, while pain and temperature take the anterolateral route.
The single sharpest discriminator between them is where they cross, the medulla versus the spinal cord.
As for the remaining spinoreticular, spinocerebellar and spino olivary tracts, they serve arousal, cerebellar coordination and motor adjustment rather than conscious perception.
And with that, we've traced the main routes that sensation takes from the body up to the brain.
