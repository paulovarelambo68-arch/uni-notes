## Dorsal and Ventral Visual Pathways

Beyond the primary visual cortex, or V one, vision is sorted into two largely separate cortical pathways.
The magnocellular pathway continues as the dorsal pathway to the posterior parietal cortex, and it serves spatial vision.
The parvocellular pathway, on the other hand, continues as the ventral pathway to the inferior temporal cortex, and it serves object recognition.
We'll trace each stream from start to finish, and after that we'll see where the two of them lie on the brain.

## The magnocellular pathway

Our first stream is the magnocellular pathway, which carries the output of the M ganglion cells onwards from V one.
It relays from V one to visual area two, or V two, and on to visual area three, or V three.
Finally, it reaches the middle temporal area, known as M T.
From V one, it forms the dorsal pathway, sweeping up into the posterior parietal cortex.
Along the way, each area adds its own analysis.
V three processes the orientation and position of objects, whereas M T processes object movement.
Taken as a whole, the stream provides spatial vision, meaning the detection of motion and change in the visual scene.

## The parvocellular pathway

The second stream, the parvocellular pathway, carries the output of the P ganglion cells onwards from V one.
This time the relay runs from V one to V two and on to visual area four, or V four.
It forms the ventral pathway, which sweeps down into the inferior temporal cortex.
For its part, V four analyses colour, form, shape and texture.
Overall, the stream provides object recognition, that is, the perception of form and detail.
V two itself, meanwhile, has no separate speciality.
Like every extrastriate area, it contains its own map of visual space, and it is largely dependent on V one for its activation.
What it does do is serve as the shared relay of both pathways, passing information on to the specialised areas that follow.

## The two pathways on the brain

Now let's place the two streams on the brain itself.
V one sits at the occipital pole, and both pathways begin there before passing through V two, just anterior to it.
The dorsal pathway runs from V one through V two to M T, at the junction of the occipital and temporal lobes, and then up into the parietal lobe.
This is the route for spatial vision.
The ventral pathway runs from V one through V two to V four, and then curves down and forwards into the temporal lobe.
This one is the route for object recognition.
The anatomical split therefore mirrors the functional one.
Put simply, dorsal and parietal is for where things are and how they move, while ventral and temporal is for what things are.

## Summary

Bringing everything together, the magnocellular pathway relays from V one through V two and V three to M T.
It forms the dorsal pathway to the posterior parietal cortex.
In this stream, V three encodes orientation and position, and M T encodes object movement, so the stream gives spatial vision, motion and change.
The parvocellular pathway relays from V one through V two to V four, and it forms the ventral pathway to the inferior temporal cortex.
Within it, V four encodes colour, form, shape and texture, and the stream gives object recognition, form and detail.
And with that, both streams have been followed from V one to their destinations in the parietal and temporal cortex.
