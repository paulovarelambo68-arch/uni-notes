## Glia in Synaptogenesis and Synaptic Activity

Glia are not passive support cells, but active partners at the synapse.
In the central nervous system, or C N S, synapses only form and persist when astrocytes or other macroglia are present.
On top of that, glia sense and respond to ongoing synaptic activity.
In what follows, we'll look first at how glia help synapses form, and then at the part they play in synaptic activity.

## 1. Role of glia in synaptogenesis

We'll begin with development, when neurons migrate into position.
Yet synapse formation only occurs when astrocytes or other macroglia are present.
The astrocytes release extracellular protein signals, and these signals trigger synapse formation in the C N S.
This dependence isn't limited to development, either.
Adult hippocampal stem cells need astrocytes to form synapses as well.
Even once synapses are built, they still rely on glia.
That's because macroglia are necessary for synapse maintenance, not only for formation.

## 2. Macroglia actively control synaptogenesis

Crucially, the glial control of synapse formation is active rather than permissive.
Astrocytes and other macroglia regulate synapse number, synapse stability and synapse function.
Nor does this principle stop at the C N S.
There is evidence that Schwann cells, out in the periphery, trigger the formation of the neuromuscular junction.

## 3. Role in synaptic activity

Turning to synaptic activity, glia take part in ongoing signalling too.
In practice, the glia involved are astrocytes, as has been shown in culture.
They can do this because glial cells carry neurotransmitter receptors, mostly metabotropic ones that are coupled to intracellular cascades.
When transmitter is released during synaptic transmission, the glial cell responds as well.
Its response shows up as calcium transient currents, which are brief rises of intracellular calcium inside the glial cell.
These transients are the measurable sign that the glia has sensed synaptic activity.
From there, the calcium works as a second messenger within the glial cell.
The signal can even propagate as a calcium wave, both within one glial cell and between neighbouring glia.
As for the purpose of that calcium signal, it triggers the glial cell to release gliotransmitters in response to neuronal activity.
What these gliotransmitters signal back onto is the synapse itself.
They can regulate how much transmitter the presynaptic terminal releases, or alter the responsiveness of the postsynaptic cell.
Through this, they can also influence activity-dependent plastic changes.
Put another way, synaptic signalling is not a private dialogue between two neurons.
The glial cell listens, and talks back to tune the transmission.

## Summary

So, pulling the threads together, astrocytes and other macroglia are required for both the formation and the maintenance of C N S synapses.
They drive formation through extracellular protein signals, and they actively regulate synapse number, stability and function.
Schwann cells play the equivalent role at the neuromuscular junction.
Beyond that, glia sense synaptic activity through calcium transients.
They answer it by releasing gliotransmitters, which makes them active participants at the synapse.
And with that, we've seen how glia help synapses form and last, and how they join in the conversation across them.
