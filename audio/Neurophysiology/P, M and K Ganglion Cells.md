## P, M and K Ganglion Cells

The axons of retinal ganglion cells form the optic nerve.
These cells fall into two major functional classes, the P cells and the M cells, with an additional third type known as the K ganglion cells.
The classes differ in receptive field size, in conduction speed and in the kind of visual information they carry.
These differences are the anatomical basis of the parallel pathways of vision.
We'll meet each class in turn, and then look at how the shape of each cell fits its job.

## P cells

First up are the P cells, which belong to the parvocellular division.
They are the most numerous of the ganglion cell classes.
Each one has a small receptive field, which means it samples a tiny patch of the retina with fine precision.
P cells are colour sensitive, and they underlie high visual acuity and high spatial resolution.
So the P cell stream carries the detail and the colour of the visual scene.

## M cells

Next come the M cells, which belong to the magnocellular division.
Their receptive fields are large, pooling input from a wide patch of retina.
They are fast conducting, and they are specialised for motion perception and rapidly changing detail, which gives them high temporal resolution.
Where P cells resolve fine spatial detail, M cells resolve fine timing.
In other words, the M cell stream tells the brain where things are moving and how fast the scene is changing.
As for the class names, they come from the layers of the lateral geniculate nucleus that each type feeds.
M cells project to the large celled, or magnocellular, layers of this nucleus, whereas P cells project to its small celled, or parvocellular, layers.
Each division therefore names the pathway the cell drives, not the cell itself.

## K ganglion cells

Beyond these two major classes, K ganglion cells are an additional type, identified as transmitting aspects of colour.
As noted in class, they carry blue light information.
This complements the colour sensitivity of the P cell stream.

## Morphology of the three classes

Turning now to shape, the P ganglion cell has a small, compact dendritic tree, which matches its small receptive field.
The M ganglion cell, by contrast, has a much larger, widely branching tree, matching its large receptive field.
The K ganglion cell sits between the two in size, with a sparse and irregular pattern of branching.
In this way, the functional contrast between the classes is visible in the anatomy itself.
Put simply, dendritic tree size sets receptive field size, and that in turn sets what each class is good at detecting.

## Summary

To sum up, ganglion cell axons form the optic nerve and belong to two major classes.
P cells of the parvocellular division are numerous, with small receptive fields and colour sensitivity, along with high visual acuity and high spatial resolution.
M cells of the magnocellular division have large receptive fields and conduct fast.
They handle motion perception and rapidly changing detail, with high temporal resolution.
Lastly, K ganglion cells are an additional type that transmits aspects of colour, notably blue light information.
And that rounds off the three classes of retinal ganglion cell.
