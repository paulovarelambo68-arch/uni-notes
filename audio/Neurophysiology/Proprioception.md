## Proprioception

Proprioception is the perception of joint position, joint movement and the velocity of joint movement.
It also takes in the unconscious sensations that guide posture, reflexes and movement without ever reaching awareness.
All of this depends on low-threshold mechanoreceptors embedded in the musculoskeletal system itself.
There are three kinds, namely muscle spindles, Golgi tendon organs and joint receptors.
Together they supply detailed and continuous information about where the limbs are in space, which is essential for performing complex movements accurately.
We'll go through the three receptors one by one, beginning with the spindle.

## Muscle spindles

Muscle spindles are found in virtually all skeletal muscles, and they provide the most detailed proprioceptive signal.
Each spindle is a connective tissue capsule containing four to eight specialised fibres, known as intrafusal fibres.
The spindle lies among the ordinary, force-producing extrafusal fibres, and its intrafusal fibres are arranged in parallel with them.
Sensory endings are coiled around the central region of the intrafusal fibres.
When the muscle is stretched, mechanically gated ion channels open in these endings and trigger action potentials.
Two classes of afferent carry this signal.
The large group one A primary endings adapt rapidly, and they report the velocity and direction of movement.
The group two secondary endings, on the other hand, give sustained responses that report static muscle length, and therefore limb position.
Beyond its sensory role, the spindle is also under motor control.
Gamma motor neurons adjust the tension of the intrafusal fibres, so that the spindle remains sensitive at all muscle lengths.
Alpha motor neurons, meanwhile, drive the extrafusal fibres that produce force.

## Golgi tendon organs

Moving on, the second receptor is the Golgi tendon organ, an encapsulated receptor sitting at the junction between muscle and tendon.
Its sensory ending is woven among the collagen fascicles of the tendon, which places it in series with the muscle fibres.
As a result, it is deformed whenever the muscle develops tension.
It reports changes in muscle tension to the spinal cord through a group one B afferent.
Put simply, where the spindle measures length, the tendon organ measures force.

## Joint receptors

The third type, the joint receptors, resemble the mechanoreceptors of the skin, such as Ruffini endings and Pacinian corpuscles.
They were once thought to be the main source of proprioceptive information.
In fact, they are not.
Joint receptors play only a minor role in proprioception.
Patients with artificial joint replacements show only minor deficits in judging limb position and movement.
Likewise, anaesthetising a joint such as the knee does not affect these judgements at all.
Their genuine contributions are more specific.
First, they are important for judging finger position.
Here they work alongside cutaneous Ruffini afferents and muscle spindle input, giving the fingers their fine representation.
Second, they serve a protective role, signalling positions that lie near the limits of the joint's range of motion.

## Summary

Drawing the threads together, proprioception is the perception of joint position, joint movement and the velocity of movement, including its unconscious components.
It is carried by low-threshold mechanoreceptors.
Muscle spindles are capsules of intrafusal fibres lying parallel to the extrafusal fibres.
They are innervated by group one A and group two afferents, and they are set by gamma motor neurons.
Spindles report muscle length and the speed of stretch.
Golgi tendon organs, by contrast, are encapsulated receptors among the collagen of the tendon, served by group one B afferents, and they report muscle tension.
Lastly, joint receptors resemble the mechanoreceptors of the skin but play only a minor role in proprioception.
They matter for judging finger position, and for protectively signalling the limits of joint range of motion.
And that is proprioception, the body's continuous account of where the limbs are and how they move.
