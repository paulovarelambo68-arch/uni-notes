## REM versus Slow Wave Sleep

Sleep comes in two contrasting states.
The first is rapid eye movement sleep, known as REM sleep, and the second is slow wave sleep.
The two are defined by their signatures on the electroencephalogram, and by their opposite physiological profiles.
Slow wave sleep is a state of bodily rest, with a regular autonomic rhythm.
REM sleep, on the other hand, combines muscle paralysis with autonomic activation.
We'll take slow wave sleep first, and then turn to REM sleep.

## Slow Wave Sleep

During slow wave sleep, a substantial amount of muscle tone remains, although that tone is reduced compared with wakefulness.
Body movements also remain frequent.
Heart rate, blood pressure and respiration are all maintained at a regular rate.
In other words, autonomic activity stays steady throughout this stage.
As for the stages involved, only N three, the deepest of the three non-REM stages, is considered slow wave sleep.

## REM Sleep

REM sleep looks very different, starting with a pronounced loss of muscle tone.
As a result, the body is effectively paralysed.
Heart rate, blood pressure and respiration show sharp fluctuations, rather than a regular rhythm.
The rapid eye movements themselves are what give this stage its name.
Brain temperature is raised as well, compared with slow wave sleep.
Finally, it has been suggested that dreams might reflect exactly those sharp autonomic fluctuations.
So slow wave sleep pairs a resting body with a steady autonomic rhythm, while REM sleep pairs a paralysed body with fluctuating autonomic activity.
