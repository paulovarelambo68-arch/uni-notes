## Referred Pain

Referred pain is pain that arises in the internal organs but is perceived instead on the surface of the body.
We'll look first at the convergence theory proposed to explain it, and then at the classic examples of where each organ's pain is felt.

## Convergence theory

The proposed mechanism is known as convergence.
Nociceptors from several locations, both visceral and somatic, converge on a single ascending tract in the spinal cord.
In other words, a primary sensory neuron from an organ and one from a region of skin synapse on the same secondary sensory neuron.
From there, the ascending path of that secondary neuron runs up to the somatosensory cortex.
Now, pain signals from the skin are more common than pain from the internal organs.
For that reason, the brain associates activation of that pathway with pain in the skin.
As a result, the visceral signal is felt on the body surface rather than in the organ itself.

## Classic examples

Turning to the classic examples, the heart refers pain to the chest and the left arm.
Pain from the liver and the gall bladder is referred to the shoulder region.
Next, the stomach refers pain to the upper central abdomen.
The small intestine refers to the umbilical region, while the colon refers to the lower central abdomen.
The appendix refers to the periumbilical region.
Finally, the ureters refer to the groin.
In each case, the organ and its referral site share the same level of entry into the spinal cord.
That shared level is what allows their nociceptors to converge.
One further point came up in class, beyond the slides.
The classic cardiac pattern of chest and left arm pain comes mostly from studies in men.
Women having a heart attack more often report less typical distributions of pain.
It's a reminder that much of our knowledge of pain carries a male bias.

## Summary

In summary, referred pain is visceral pain that is perceived on the body surface.
Visceral and somatic nociceptors converge on the same ascending tract in the spinal cord.
Because cutaneous pain is more common, the brain attributes the signal to the skin.
As for the classic referrals, the heart refers to the chest and left arm, and the liver and gall bladder to the shoulder region.
The stomach, small intestine and colon refer to successive bands of the central abdomen.
Lastly, the appendix refers to the periumbilical region, and the ureters to the groin.
And that's referred pain, a signal from the organs that the brain reads as coming from the skin.
