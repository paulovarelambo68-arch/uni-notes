## Sensory Modalities

A sensory modality is each unique type of sensation.
In other words, it is every distinct quality of experience, such as touch, pain or vision, that the nervous system detects through its own class of receptor.
We'll begin with the somatic modalities, and then move on to the special senses and the visceral ones.

## Somatic modalities

The somatic modalities gather information from the skin and from the deep tissues.
Touch, pressure and vibration are mechanical stimuli, and they are detected by mechanoreceptors.
These receptors are distributed through the epidermis, the dermis and the deep tissue, so mechanical energy is sampled at different depths.
Alongside touch, pressure and vibration sits proprioception, which is the sense of the position and movement of the body.
Then there is temperature, detected by thermoreceptors, and finally pain, detected by nociceptors.

## Special senses and visceral modalities

Now, the same receptor logic extends to the special senses and to the viscera.
Mechanical stimuli are also detected by hair cells, which mediate hearing and balance.
Baroreceptors detect mechanical stimuli as well, and they monitor blood pressure.
Electromagnetic radiation, meanwhile, is detected by the rods and cones.
Last of all, chemoreceptors cover taste and smell.
They also cover internal chemical variables, such as glucose and the partial pressures of oxygen and carbon dioxide.
That rounds off the sensory modalities, each one matched to its own class of receptor.
