## The Functions of Sleep

Sleep has no single agreed function.
Its purposes are therefore inferred largely from what happens when it is lost.
Two states are central here, slow wave sleep and rapid eye movement sleep, which is known as REM sleep.
Both show a rebound increase following deprivation, and that rebound is the key evidence that both states are needed.
What's more, each stage apparently answers a different need.
We'll weigh up the evidence that sleep is needed, look at what REM sleep does, and then finish with slow wave sleep.

## Evidence that sleep is needed

Consider first what happens when sleep is lost.
The missing stages are repaid at the next opportunity.
Both slow wave sleep and REM sleep show a rebound increase following deprivation, rising above their usual levels.
Such compensation implies that each stage is regulated and required, rather than optional.

## REM sleep

Turning now to REM sleep, selective deprivation of this state causes abnormalities in sensory processing.
Subtle emotional and personality disturbances follow as well.
These deficits point to a role in mind maintenance.
REM brings repeated bouts of cortical activation, close to waking levels.
The proposal is that this activity keeps the machinery of perception, emotion and personality in working order.
After all, these are precisely the faculties that falter when REM is lost.
REM is also proposed to be necessary for the consolidation of memories.
Removing REM after learning impairs later recall.
That is evidence that memory traces laid down while awake are stabilised during these episodes.

## Slow wave sleep

Slow wave sleep, by contrast, is the phase linked to body maintenance.
Within it, the deepest slow wave sleep is the part that really matters.
Three things happen during slow wave sleep.
First, hormones that stimulate protein synthesis are released, driving growth and tissue repair.
Second, glymphatic clearance increases, washing metabolic waste out of the brain.
Third, the immune system is stimulated, which aids defence and recovery.
Consistent with this restorative role, the stage called N three is increased after exercise.
N three is the deepest stage in the current three-stage nomenclature for non-REM sleep.
It merges the former stages three and four.
In other words, the stage four of older usage is the N three of today.
Its rise after physical exertion ties the deepest sleep directly to the recovery needs of the body.

## Summary

Taken together, both states rebound after deprivation, yet they do different work.
REM sleep serves the mind.
Its loss brings abnormalities in sensory processing, along with subtle emotional and personality disturbances.
It is credited with both mind maintenance and memory consolidation.
Slow wave sleep, meanwhile, serves the body.
It does so through hormones that stimulate protein synthesis, through glymphatic clearance and through immune stimulation.
Its deepest stage, N three, rises after exercise.
In short, REM sleep tends the mind, while slow wave sleep tends the body.
