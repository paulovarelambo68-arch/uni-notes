
>The receptors that B cells carry on their surface to catch antigen, ==the B cell receptors (BCR), are antibodies==. Once activated, B cells secrete antibodies with ==the same specificity as their BCR==, in other words antibodies that recognise the same antigen. Each antibody is a ==Y-shaped protein==: its ==two arms, called Fab, catch the antigen==, and its ==stem, called Fc, attaches to other immune cells== and switches them on. Between them, arms and stem give antibodies three jobs: ==blocking toxins and microbes by binding them directly (neutralisation)==, ==targeting microbes to immune cells for destruction== and ==activating complement==. Antibodies come in ==five classes, called isotypes (IgG, IgM, IgA, IgD and IgE)==, and the one ==abundant in saliva and other secretions is IgA==.

### 1. B cell receptors are antibodies

In the Respond step of adaptive immunity, B cells (B lymphocytes) make antibodies, which act in the blood and other body fluids, so ==antibody defence is called humoral immunity==, from humour, an old word for a body fluid. Antibodies are also called ==immunoglobulins (Ig)==, meaning immune globulins, a family of plasma proteins, and this is the Ig at the start of IgG, IgA and the other antibody classes. Antibodies first appear on the B cell itself, because ==B cell receptors (BCR) are antibodies==, held in its membrane with their antigen-catching arms facing out.

When these receptors bind their antigen, the B cell is activated, boosted by helper T cells ([[T Cells and Antigen Presentation]]), and divides again and again into a clone of identical B cells. Some of these turn into plasma cells, which release antibodies into the blood (see the clonal selection figure in [[Adaptive Immunity – Principles and Clonal Selection]]). These antibodies have ==the same specificity as the BCR==, which means they recognise exactly the same antigen, because they carry the same antigen-catching tips as the receptor.

### 2. The shape of an antibody

The receptor and the secreted antibody share the same Y-shaped design, built from protein chains. A polypeptide chain is a single string of amino acids, and a protein can be made of one such chain or of several joined together. ==An antibody has four polypeptide chains, two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains==, heavy and light simply meaning larger and smaller. The two heavy chains run side by side to form the stem of the Y and then separate to form the inner half of each arm, while each light chain lies along the outer half of one arm.

Along its length, ==each chain has a variable (V) region and a constant (C) region==. The variable region is the end at the tip of an arm, and each B cell clone makes its own version of it, shaped to fit that clone's antigen, which is what gives the antibody its specificity. Along its length, ==each chain has a variable (V) region and a constant (C) region==. The variable region, at the tip of an arm, differs from one B cell clone to the next, whereas the constant region, the rest of the chain, comes in only a few standard versions.

The variable regions give rise to the ==Fab (antigen-binding fragments)==, the two arms. The variable tips of each arm form an antigen-binding site shaped to fit the clone's own antigen, which gives ==highly specific binding to antigen==, and because the two arms are identical, ==one antibody has two antigen-binding sites==. The constant regions give rise to the ==Fc (crystallisable fragment)==, the stem, which ==binds to receptors on immune cells to trigger their activation==. The names come from experiments that cut antibodies into fragments, in which the stem fragment formed crystals. In short, the arms recognise and the stem recruits.

### 3. What antibodies do

This division of labour gives antibodies three functions. The first, ==neutralisation of toxins and microbes by direct binding==, needs only the arms, which cover the parts a toxin or microbe uses to attach to and enter cells.

The second, ==targeting of microbes to immune cells for destruction==, needs the stem, because immune cells carry ==Fc receptors, which grip the Fc of an antibody==. In ==opsonisation and phagocytosis==, antibodies coat a microbe (opsonisation) and a phagocyte grips their stems and engulfs it ([[Innate Immunity – Pathogen Recognition and Phagocytosis]]). In ==antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC)==, antibodies coat a target cell, such as an infected cell, and a natural killer (NK) cell grips them and kills it ([[Complement and Natural Killer Cells]]).

The third, ==activation of complement== ([[Complement and Natural Killer Cells]]), is set off by antibodies on a microbe through the ==classical pathway== and leads to ==lysis== (bursting) of the microbe, ==phagocytosis of microbes opsonised with C3b==, a complement fragment that phagocytes grip with C3b receptors, and ==inflammation==, which draws in more immune cells.

![[Screenshot 2026-10-23 at 13.39.53.png|391]]

Every route starts with the arms binding, and all but neutralisation then depend on the stem.

### 4. The five isotypes

Since the stem is made of heavy chains, the heavy chain decides what an antibody can do. ==Five types of heavy chain, γ, μ, α, δ and ε (gamma, mu, alpha, delta, epsilon), give rise to five isotypes==, the classes of antibody named after them, ==IgG, IgM, IgA, IgD and IgE==. ==IgG, IgE and IgD each have one Y unit (monomers)==, ==IgA often has two (a dimer)== and ==IgM has five (a pentamer)==.

![[Screenshot 2026-10-23 at 13.40.16.png|350]]

The figure sets the unit counts side by side. Yellow disulfide bonds, chemical bridges between sulfur atoms, hold each single Y together. IgM is a star of five units with their stems at the centre, linked by disulfide bonds and a blue ==joining chain==, a small protein that holds units together. IgA is two units joined stem to stem by a joining chain and wrapped in a red ==secretory protein==, which protects it from digestion in secretions.

Each isotype has its own role.

- ==IgG comprises 70 to 75% of circulating immunoglobulins==, has ==four subclasses (IgG1 to IgG4)== and is the ==only Ig transported across the placenta==, so it protects the newborn, whose own immunity is immature.
- ==IgM is the first antibody produced in response to a new infection==, and, as a monomer, it sits ==with IgD on the surface of many B cells as a B cell receptor==.
- ==IgA is abundant in secretions, including saliva==, where it stops microbes before they enter the body, which is why exercise studies measure salivary IgA.
- ==IgD comprises less than 1% of serum Ig== and serves with IgM as a B cell receptor.
- ==IgE features in allergic responses== and is ==important in defence against helminth (parasitic worm) infection==, alongside mast cells and eosinophils ([[Cells and Tissues of the Immune System]]).

IgM comes first in a response and IgG later, a shift picked up in [[Immunological Memory – Primary and Secondary Responses]].

| Isotype | Number of units | Where found or special property |
|---|---|---|
| IgG (γ) | 1 | 70 to 75% of circulating Ig; four subclasses; crosses the placenta |
| IgM (μ) | 5 (1 as B cell receptor) | First in a new infection; B cell receptor with IgD |
| IgA (α) | Often 2 | Secretions, including saliva |
| IgD (δ) | 1 | Under 1% of serum Ig; B cell receptor with IgM |
| IgE (ε) | 1 | Allergy; defence against helminths |

### Summary

>B cell receptors are antibodies (immunoglobulins), and after activation and clonal expansion, plasma cells release ==antibodies with the same specificity as the BCR==. Each antibody is a Y of ==two heavy and two light chains==: ==variable regions form two antigen-binding Fab arms== and ==constant regions form the Fc stem, which binds Fc receptors on immune cells==. Antibodies ==neutralise toxins and microbes, target microbes for phagocytosis or ADCC, and activate complement by the classical pathway==. ==Five heavy chains give five isotypes==, IgG, IgE and IgD being monomers, IgA often a dimer and IgM a pentamer. ==IgG (70 to 75% of circulating Ig) alone crosses the placenta==, ==IgM comes first in infection== and partners IgD (under 1% of serum Ig) on B cells, ==IgA is abundant in saliva== and ==IgE features in allergy and helminth defence==.
