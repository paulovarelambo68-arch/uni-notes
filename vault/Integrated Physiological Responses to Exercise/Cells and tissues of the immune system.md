
> Immune cells are ==formed and mature in the primary lymphoid organs (bone marrow and thymus)== and are ==concentrated and function in the secondary ones (lymph nodes, spleen and mucosal immune system)==. All come from one marrow stem cell by ==haematopoiesis==, which splits them into a ==myeloid and a lymphoid family==. Some circulate, while ==dendritic cells, macrophages and mast cells are resident sentinels==. ==Only B and T cells are adaptive==, so all the others, ==including neutrophils and natural killer cells, are innate==.

### 1. Where immune cells are made and mature: the primary lymphoid organs

The cells of both branches met in [[Immune System Overview – Functions and the Two Branches]] are produced and gathered in lymphoid tissues. Lymphoid means made of, or housing, lymphocytes. These are a kind of white blood cell, chiefly the B and T cells, while the other kinds, such as neutrophils, appear in section 3. The ==primary lymphoid organs==, the ==bone marrow and thymus==, are where immune cells are ==formed and mature==, that is, become ready to work. ==All immune cells are produced in the bone marrow==, where ==B cells also mature==, while T cells leave it early and ==mature in the thymus==, a gland behind the breastbone.

### 2. Where immune cells gather and work: the secondary (peripheral) lymphoid organs

Once mature, the cells spread out to the ==secondary (peripheral) lymphoid organs==, where they are ==concentrated and function==: the ==lymph nodes, spleen and mucosal immune system==. Lymph nodes ==filter lymph==, the tissue fluid draining back towards the blood, and are ==where lymphocytes meet antigen==; while the spleen ==filters the blood==. The mucosal immune system is lymphoid tissue under the mucosa, the moist mucous membranes lining the gut, airways and urogenital tract, through which most microbes enter. The figure labels it ==MALT, GALT and BALT (mucosa-, gut- and bronchus-associated lymphoid tissue)==, with the tonsils and adenoids and the urogenital lymphoid tissue as further examples.

### 3. Haematopoiesis: one stem cell, two families

Back in the marrow, production has a name: ==haematopoiesis==, the formation of blood cells. All blood cells, immune cells included, come from ==one haematopoietic stem cell==, which gives rise to two progenitors. The ==common myeloid progenitor== (myeloid meaning the non-lymphocyte family) produces ==erythrocytes (red cells), platelets and the white cells called monocytes (which become macrophages), neutrophils, eosinophils and basophils==. The ==common lymphoid progenitor== produces the lymphocytes. B cells end as ==plasma cells==, which secrete antibodies, and ==memory cells==, which remember an antigen, while T cells end as ==helper, cytotoxic (killer) and regulatory T cells==, the last labelled ==suppressor==, its older name, on the figure. The figure leaves out ==natural killer (NK) cells, which are lymphocytes too==, and ==dendritic cells and mast cells==, tissue guards met in the next section, which ==arise mainly from the myeloid family==.

Skip the intermediate stages (myeloblast, promonocyte and so on), because the point is that every immune cell traces back to one stem cell.

### 4. Cells that circulate and cells that stand guard

Once made, immune cells follow one of two lifestyles: some circulate between the blood and the tissues, while others are resident in the tissues. The circulating cells, which can also enter tissues, are ==neutrophils, monocytes/macrophages, eosinophils and lymphocytes==. The resident ones, ==dendritic cells, macrophages and mast cells==, are ==sentinel cells==, guards posted in the tissue to raise the alarm. Macrophages are on both lists because ==a monocyte in the blood becomes a macrophage once it settles in a tissue==.

Each sentinel has its own job. The ==dendritic cell detects pathogens and presents them to lymphocytes==, which means it catches disease-causing microbes and shows pieces of them to lymphocytes ([[T Cells and Antigen Presentation]]). The ==macrophage performs phagocytosis==, engulfing and digesting microbes ([[Innate Immunity – Pathogen Recognition and Phagocytosis]]), and the ==mast cell handles allergy and defence against helminths==, or parasitic worms.

### 5. The cast by branch

With every cell placed, the cast can now be sorted by branch. The innate cells are ==monocytes/macrophages, neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, mast cells, dendritic cells and natural killer cells==, and the adaptive cells are ==the lymphocytes, B cells and T cells==. NK cells are therefore the odd ones out, ==lymphocytes by family but innate by branch==.

The jobs can then be stated cell by cell. ==Neutrophils are the most abundant white cell and the first to arrive at an infection==, where they phagocytose microbes, and being innate they are ==present and functional from birth==, respond immediately and recognise microbes non-specifically, but ==do not develop or adapt over a lifetime==. Monocytes/macrophages ==also present antigen==, ==eosinophils and basophils act against parasites and in allergy==, and ==NK cells kill virus-infected and cancerous body cells== ([[Complement and Natural Killer Cells]]). ==B cells make antibodies== ([[B Cells and Antibodies]]) and ==T cells carry out cell-mediated immunity==, meaning defence by cells rather than by antibodies ([[T Cells and Antigen Presentation]]).

| Cell | Branch | Circulating or resident | Main job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neutrophil | Innate | Circulating | Most abundant, first to arrive at infection, phagocytosis |
| Monocyte/macrophage | Innate | Monocyte circulates, macrophage resident | Phagocytosis, antigen presentation |
| Eosinophil | Innate | Circulating | Defence against parasites, allergy |
| Basophil | Innate | Circulating | Defence against parasites, allergy |
| Mast cell | Innate | Resident sentinel | Allergy, defence against helminths |
| Dendritic cell | Innate | Resident sentinel | Detects pathogens, presents them to lymphocytes |
| NK cell | Innate (a lymphocyte) | Circulating | Kills virus-infected and cancerous cells |
| B cell | Adaptive | Circulating | Makes antibodies |
| T cell | Adaptive | Circulating | Cell-mediated immunity |

### Summary

> Immune cells are made in the ==bone marrow==, where ==B cells also mature==, while ==T cells mature in the thymus==, these being the ==primary lymphoid organs==. They work in the ==secondary lymphoid organs==: ==lymph nodes, which filter lymph, the spleen, which filters blood, and the mucosal immune system==. ==Haematopoiesis== gives a ==myeloid family== (erythrocytes, platelets, monocytes, neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, and mainly dendritic and mast cells) and a ==lymphoid family== (B, T and NK cells). ==Neutrophils, monocytes/macrophages, eosinophils and lymphocytes circulate==, while ==dendritic cells, macrophages and mast cells are resident sentinels==. ==Only B and T cells are adaptive==, so ==neutrophils, NK cells and the rest are innate==.
