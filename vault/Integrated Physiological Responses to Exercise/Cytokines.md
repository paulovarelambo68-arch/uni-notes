
>Cytokines are ==small proteins== that cells secrete to signal to themselves (==autocrine==) or to their neighbours (==paracrine==) through ==specific receptors==. Each immune cell's ==cytokine profile can determine its function==, and acting together as ==a network==, cytokines ==drive and orchestrate immune responses==. ==Pro-inflammatory== cytokines push inflammation up and ==anti-inflammatory== ones damp it down.

### 1. What a cytokine is

Immune cells (see [[Cells and Tissues of the Immune System]]) talk to each other through cytokines, which are ==small proteins of 5 to 20 [[kilodaltons]] (kDa)==. Cytokines are ==secreted by cells== and act on the cell that made them (==autocrine==) or on cells nearby (==paracrine==), but only on cells that carry their ==specific receptor==.

It's important not to confuse cytokines with complement, a set of ==plasma proteins that circulate in an inactive state until a pathogen activates them==. Cytokines, by contrast, are ==made by cells when needed== and carry messages between cells.

Cytokines themselves fall into four families: ==interleukins (IL-1 to IL-22)==, ==interferons (IFN-α, β, γ)==, ==tumour necrosis factors (TNF-α, β)== and ==transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β)==. The names record what each family was first seen to do: signal between leukocytes (the white blood cells), interfere with viruses, kill tumours and alter cell growth.

Whatever the family, each immune cell secretes ==a characteristic profile of cytokines that can determine that cell's function==. Cytokines are therefore ==important effectors of immune function==, meaning that cells do much of their work through them. They ==drive and orchestrate immune responses==, switching cells on, calling them to a site and telling them when to stop.

### 2. Pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokines

Starting and stopping a response are opposite jobs, so cytokines are usually grouped by their effect on inflammation. Inflammation is the local reaction that brings fluid and leukocytes from the blood into injured or infected tissue. 

| Cytokine | Group | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| IL-1 | Pro-inflammatory | Makes local blood vessels let leukocytes out, and causes fever |
| TNF-α | Pro-inflammatory | Acts like IL-1, and activates neutrophils |
| IL-6 | Pro-inflammatory (see below) | Makes the liver release C-reactive protein, a blood marker of inflammation |
| IFN-γ | Pro-inflammatory | Activates macrophages to kill what they engulf |
| IL-10 | Anti-inflammatory | Switches macrophages off |
| IL-1ra | Anti-inflammatory | The IL-1 receptor antagonist, which blocks IL-1's receptor |
| TGF-β | Anti-inflammatory | Holds back lymphocytes and macrophages |

Calling cells to a site falls to the ==chemokines==, cytokines that ==attract leukocytes to where they are needed==. ==IL-6 is the odd one out==, with ==both a pro-inflammatory and an anti-inflammatory face==, which is the subject of [[IL-6 in Exercise]]. T cells are a clear example of how the cytokines a cell releases decide what it does. ==Regulatory T cells== suppress immune responses and ==make the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10==, while helper T cells release cytokines that boost B cells, macrophages and other T cells ([[T Cells and Antigen Presentation]]).

Each arrow runs from the cell that produces the cytokines in its box to the cell they act on. For example, the arrow from macrophage to neutrophil (IL-1, IL-8, TNF-α) shows macrophages calling in neutrophils. The loops back to the same cell are autocrine signals. Do not memorise the lists in the boxes. The message is that ==every cell type both sends and receives many cytokines==. They therefore form ==a web rather than a chain==, in which ==one activated cell can recruit and instruct many others==.

### Summary

>Cytokines are ==small proteins (5 to 20 kDa)== that cells secrete to act in an ==autocrine or paracrine== manner through ==specific receptors==. Their ==four families are interleukins (IL-1 to IL-22), interferons (IFN-α, β, γ), tumour necrosis factors (TNF-α, β) and TGF-β==. ==IL-1, TNF-α, IFN-γ and usually IL-6== are pro-inflammatory, ==IL-10, IL-1ra and TGF-β== are anti-inflammatory, and ==chemokines attract cells to a site==. Each cell's ==cytokine profile can determine its function==, and together cytokines form ==a network that drives and orchestrates immune responses==.
