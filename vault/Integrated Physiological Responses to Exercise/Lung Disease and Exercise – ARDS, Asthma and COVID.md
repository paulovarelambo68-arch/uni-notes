
> The lungs can fail in different ways, and knowing how each failure works explains who struggles to breathe and why. This note follows three of them: lungs that flood with fluid, airways that narrow, and the lingering effects of COVID on fitness.

### 1. When the alveoli flood: ARDS

Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) is a sudden lung failure in which the alveoli, the air sacs where oxygen enters the blood, ==fill with fluid==. Its commonest trigger is pneumonia, caused mostly by bacteria such as Streptococcus pneumoniae or viruses such as influenza, and rarely by fungi or parasites.

The infection sets off a surge of neutrophils and cytokines, the immune cells and chemical signals of inflammation [[Lung Immune Cells – Toll-like Receptors, Macrophages and Neutrophils]]. They injure the alveolar-capillary membrane, the thin barrier between air and blood. Fluid then leaks from the capillaries into the air spaces (==pulmonary oedema==), so air can no longer reach the blood there [[The Respiratory Gases – Composition of Air, Partial Pressures and Exchange across the Alveolar Membrane]].

Doctors diagnose ARDS with the ==Berlin consensus definition==, which has three parts. The first is an ==acute onset==, within about a week of the trigger. The second compares the oxygen reaching the blood with the oxygen breathed in. This is the ratio ==PaO2/FiO2==, where PaO2 is the oxygen pressure in arterial blood and FiO2 is the fraction of inhaled air that is oxygen.

Room air has an FiO2 of 0.21, and a healthy lung breathing it reaches a PaO2 of about 100 mmHg. Its ratio is therefore roughly 100 divided by 0.21, about 475 mmHg. In ARDS the ratio is ==300 mmHg or less==. Blood passing flooded alveoli never meets the inhaled air, so even extra oxygen raises the PaO2 far less than it should.

The third part is a chest X-ray with white shadows in both lungs (==bilateral infiltrates==), consistent with pulmonary oedema.

![[Pulm5_slide28_ards_chest_xray.png]]

Air lets X-rays through and shows black, while fluid blocks them and shows white. Healthy lungs therefore look dark, but in this patient both lungs are hazy white, like a sponge soaked with water.

Because the diagnosis is applied inconsistently, it is hard to say how common ARDS is (its prevalence). Estimates are about ==3 to 6 per 100,000 person-years== in Europe and 1.5 to 5.3 worldwide. In other words, a few people in every 100,000 develop it each year.

New cases (the incidence) appear to be rising, yet the share of patients who die (the mortality) is slowly falling, for four reasons.

- ARDS is ==diagnosed better==.
- Treatments such as antibiotics now ==target its cause==.
- Supportive care, which keeps the patient alive while the lung heals, is ==better and starts earlier==.
- Artificial ventilation is ==gentler==. In a flooded lung only some alveoli are still open, and large, forceful machine breaths over-stretch them and injure the lung further.

### 2. When the airways narrow: asthma

Asthma narrows the airways instead of flooding the alveoli. It affects ==7 per cent of Irish adults and 25 per cent of Irish children==, and up to half of them are not well controlled.

Many attacks are set off by allergens such as ==house dust mite, pollen, mould spores and animal dander==, the skin flakes shed by pets. Exercise can itself tighten the airways (==exercise-induced bronchospasm==), because fast breathing cools and dries their lining, especially in cold, dry air [[Limits to Ventilation – Work of Breathing, Breathing Reserve, Exercise-Induced Hypoxaemia, Smoking and Cold]].

![[Pulm5_slide30_asthma_airway.png]]

The narrowing itself happens in two ways. In the normal airway the smooth muscle around it is relaxed and the passage is wide open. In the asthmatic airway the wall is inflamed and thickened, so the passage is narrower even between attacks. In the asthmatic airway during attack the smooth muscle has also tightened, squeezing the passage to a slit that mucus can easily block. Air is then trapped in the alveoli, because the airways narrow further as the lungs empty.

Narrowed airways slow the airflow, which lowers FEV1/FVC, the share of a full forced breath out that leaves in the first second. A low ratio is the ==obstructive pattern== [[Lung Volumes and Capacities – Spirometry, FEV1 and FVC]].

Medicines target the muscle and the wall separately. Relievers such as salbutamol, sold as Ventolin, are ==short-acting beta-2 agonists==. They stimulate the adrenaline receptors on airway smooth muscle (beta-2 adrenergic receptors), so the muscle relaxes and the airway widens within minutes. Stimulating the same receptors widens some arterioles, the smallest arteries [[Vascular Resistance – Pressure Profile, Radius and Control of Arterioles]]. Preventers such as Becotide, Flixotide and Ciclesonide are ==inhaled corticosteroids==, steroid drugs that calm the inflamed wall over weeks of daily use.

At the heart of this inflammation are faulty macrophages, the immune cells that patrol the airways and swallow microbes and debris [[Lung Immune Cells – Toll-like Receptors, Macrophages and Neutrophils]]. They go wrong in five ways.

- They swallow fewer microbes (==reduced phagocytosis==), which raises the risk of infection.
- They clear away fewer dying cells (==reduced efferocytosis==), and the cells left behind keep drawing more immune cells into the lung.
- They release more of the inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 and IL-8. Already inflamed, they cannot respond to a real bacterial signal such as LPS, a molecule from the walls of bacteria.
- They make more IL-10 messenger RNA, the gene's working copy, but ==less IL-10 protein==, so the signal that switches inflammation off is missing.
- The ==M2 form==, the repair type, predominates, and its repair work remodels and scars the airway wall (fibrosis).

### 3. After COVID: what three studies found

Three studies have measured how COVID-19 affected exercise capacity. It is usually judged by VO2max, the most oxygen the body can use each minute [[Oxygen Uptake – VO2, VO2max and the Lactate Threshold]]. In 21 professional footballers in Cyprus, even mild or moderate illness left ==silent effects==, such as a lower VO2max, that lasted longer than expected. A match is played near the lactate threshold, the intensity at which lactate starts to build up. A footballer's fitness therefore depends on that threshold as well as on VO2max.

In Basel, about half of the patients tested three months after severe COVID-19 had a reduced exercise capacity. Their VO2max was below ==82 per cent of predicted==, the value expected for their age, sex and size. Yet only a minority were limited by their lungs. Most of them were limited instead by deconditioning, the fitness lost through illness and inactivity, or by the heart and circulation.

In 165 elite athletes in Hungary, a well-built training plan restored good fitness in most of them ==within three months==. The infection did not restrict their careers in the long term. Exercise itself also shapes the risk of catching respiratory infections, lowering it when moderate and raising it when very heavy [[Exercise and Infection Risk – J-Shaped Curve and Open Window]].

### Summary

> ARDS is a sudden flooding of the alveoli (==pulmonary oedema==), most often after pneumonia, because neutrophils and cytokines damage the alveolar-capillary membrane. The Berlin definition needs an acute onset, bilateral infiltrates on X-ray and a ==PaO2/FiO2 of 300 mmHg or less==, against about 475 mmHg normally. ARDS strikes about 3 to 6 per 100,000 person-years in Europe, and mortality is falling with better diagnosis, cause-targeted treatment, earlier support and ==gentler ventilation==. Asthma affects ==7 per cent of Irish adults and 25 per cent of Irish children==, and its narrowed airways give a low FEV1/FVC. Relievers (==short-acting beta-2 agonists==) relax airway muscle within minutes, while preventers (inhaled corticosteroids) damp inflammation over weeks. Asthmatic macrophages show reduced phagocytosis and efferocytosis, too little IL-10 protein and a ==predominant M2 form== that remodels the airway. After COVID, silent effects lingered in footballers, half of severely ill patients had a lower VO2max, ==mainly from deconditioning==, and most elite athletes recovered within three months.
