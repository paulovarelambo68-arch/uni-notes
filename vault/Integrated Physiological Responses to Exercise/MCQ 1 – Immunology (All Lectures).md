## Which of the following would be expected to act against a microbe within the first 12 hours after it enters the body for the first time?

- [ ] Antibodies secreted by plasma cells
- [x] Complement and phagocytes
- [ ] Effector T cells
- [ ] Memory B cells

> In the timescale figure, the innate defences (epithelial barriers, phagocytes, dendritic cells, complement and NK cells) act within hours (0 to 12), while B and T lymphocytes take days (1 to 5) to multiply into antibody producers and effector T cells. The innate branch buys time while the adaptive branch is built. See [[Immune System Overview – Functions and the Two Branches#5. Timescale how the two branches work together]].

## The classical, alternative and lectin pathways of complement activation all converge on

- [x] C3.
- [ ] C5a.
- [ ] the membrane attack complex.
- [ ] C-reactive protein.

> C3 is the central component: all three pathways lead to it, and its splitting into C3a (inflammation) and C3b (opsonisation and the terminal pathway) is what starts complement’s three functions. The membrane attack complex is the end of the terminal pathway, not the point where the pathways meet. See [[Complement and Natural Killer Cells#2. Three ways in, one hub, three ways out]].

## Natural killer cells recognise cells that have been invaded by a virus or have become cancerous because these cells

- [ ] display fragments of the virus on their MHC molecules.
- [ ] are coated with C3b.
- [x] have lost or modified surface structures.
- [ ] express Toll-like receptors.

> NK cells, innate lymphocytes that make up 10 to 15% of blood lymphocytes, detect the lost or modified surface structures of infected or cancerous cells and kill them with perforin and granzyme, without needing antigen to be presented on MHC. See [[Complement and Natural Killer Cells#4. Natural killer cells]].

## Which of the following is a function of helper T cells?

- [ ] Killing foreign cells or infected host cells with perforin and granzyme
- [ ] Suppressing the immune response by producing IL-10
- [ ] Displaying fragments of digested antigen on MHC
- [x] Producing cytokines that boost B cells, macrophages and other T cells

> Helper T cells are the organisers of the response, producing cytokines that increase the activity of B cells, macrophages and other T cells. Killing with perforin and granzyme is the cytotoxic T cell, IL-10 the regulatory T cell, and antigen display on MHC the antigen-presenting cell. See [[T Cells and Antigen Presentation#2. Three kinds of T cell]].

## Antibody produced in the secondary response tends to be more specific for its antigen than that of the primary response. This is due to

- [x] affinity maturation.
- [ ] antigen presentation.
- [ ] clonal selection.
- [ ] antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.

> In affinity maturation, small random changes in the variable-region genes of dividing B cells make some antibodies fit better, and the B cells whose antibodies fit the antigen best are selected to expand as the response proceeds, so secondary antibody binds its antigen more tightly. Clonal selection is what picks out the lymphocyte in the first place, in both responses. See [[Immunological Memory – Primary and Secondary Responses#3. The secondary response]].

## Cortisol release increases during exercise. Cortisol is released from the

- [ ] adrenal medulla.
- [x] adrenal cortex.
- [ ] anterior pituitary.
- [ ] hypothalamus.

> The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis ends with cortisol from the adrenal cortex, and immune cells carry glucocorticoid receptors for it; adrenaline and noradrenaline come from the adrenal medulla under sympathetic drive. Cortisol and the catecholamines are the stress hormones that are raised by the very intense or prolonged exercise that can open the window. See [[Mechanisms of Exercise-Induced Immune Changes#1. Three messengers]].

## Acute moderate exercise typically

- [ ] creates an ‘open window’ of immune suppression lasting from 3 to 72 hours afterwards.
- [ ] causes lymphocytopenia and reduced NK cell cytotoxicity for several hours.
- [x] enhances immune surveillance, with more NK cell activity and T cell mobilisation.
- [ ] raises baseline CRP and pro-inflammatory cytokines over the following days.

> The lecture summary: acute moderate exercise enhances immune surveillance (increased NK cell activity, mobilisation of T cells, a transient rise in IL-6 and IL-15, anti-inflammatory effects), whereas acute very intense or prolonged exercise can open the window, with lymphocytopenia, reduced NK cytotoxicity and higher cortisol and catecholamines. See [[Chronic Exercise and Immune Function – Moderate versus High Intensity#5. The whole picture]] and [[Exercise and Infection Risk – J-Shaped Curve and Open Window#5. The open window hypothesis]].

## Which of the following is a metabolic effect of IL-6 released from contracting muscle during acute exercise?

- [ ] Decreased fat oxidation
- [ ] Reduced GLP-1 secretion
- [ ] Increased TNF-α production by immune cells
- [x] Enhanced glucose uptake by muscle

> Muscle-derived IL-6 acts as a metabolic regulator: it enhances glucose uptake by muscle and fat oxidation, and acute elevations stimulate GLP-1 secretion from intestinal cells and pancreatic α-cells, improving insulin secretion and glycaemic control. These effects can indirectly support the energy needs of immune cells. See [[IL-6 in Exercise#3. Metabolic effects in acute exercise]].
