Eight multiple-choice questions on Immunology II: the features of adaptive immunity, B and T lymphocytes, the three Rs, clonal selection, the types of T cell, antigen presentation, antibody structure and the primary and secondary responses. Each answer comes with a short explanation and the note and section that cover it.

## Compared with the innate immune system, the adaptive immune system is

- [ ] faster to respond and more specific.
- [x] slower to respond and more specific.
- [ ] faster to respond and less specific.
- [ ] slower to respond and less specific.

> Adaptive immunity is slower to respond than the innate system, because the few lymphocytes that recognise an antigen must first multiply, but it recognises antigens with high specificity, develops throughout a lifetime and has memory. See [[Adaptive Immunity – Principles and Clonal Selection#1. The adaptive branch and its two cell types]].

## Which of the following statements is correct?

- [ ] B cells mature in the thymus and T cells mature in the bone marrow.
- [ ] Both B cells and T cells mature in the thymus.
- [ ] Both B cells and T cells mature in the bone marrow.
- [x] B cells mature in the bone marrow and T cells mature in the thymus.

> All immune cells are produced in the bone marrow, where B cells also mature, whereas T cells complete their maturation in the thymus (T for thymus). B lymphocytes produce antibodies and T lymphocytes carry out cell-mediated immunity. See [[Adaptive Immunity – Principles and Clonal Selection#1. The adaptive branch and its two cell types]].

## In the principles of adaptive immunity, which of the following is NOT one of the ways in which lymphocytes ‘Respond’?

- [ ] Cytokine secretion
- [ ] Antibody production
- [x] Phagocytosis of the pathogen
- [ ] Direct cell killing by T cells

> The three Rs are Recognise (a specific receptor, followed by clonal selection), Respond (cytokine secretion, antibody production and direct cell killing by T cells) and Remember (memory cells). Phagocytosis is an innate function of neutrophils, macrophages and dendritic cells, not of lymphocytes. See [[Adaptive Immunity – Principles and Clonal Selection#2. The three Rs]].

## According to clonal selection, which of the following statements is INCORRECT?

- [x] There are large numbers of lymphocytes of any one specificity before the antigen is met.
- [ ] Each lymphocyte has receptors that bind a single antigen.
- [ ] The lymphocyte repertoire is extremely diverse.
- [ ] An activated lymphocyte divides rapidly to make many copies, effector cells and memory cells.

> Because the repertoire is so diverse, there are very few cells of any one specificity, each highly specific. It is clonal expansion after the antigen has been found that produces the large number of antigen-specific lymphocytes. See [[Adaptive Immunity – Principles and Clonal Selection#3. Clonal selection]].

## Which type of T cell suppresses the immune response by producing the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10?

- [ ] Helper T cell
- [x] Regulatory T cell
- [ ] Cytotoxic T cell
- [ ] Memory T cell

> Regulatory T cells (Tregs) are the brakes of the immune response and produce IL-10. Helper T cells also produce cytokines, but ones that increase the activity of B cells, macrophages and other T cells, and cytotoxic T cells kill with perforin and granzyme. See [[T Cells and Antigen Presentation#2. Three kinds of T cell]].

## Which of the following is NOT one of the antigen-presenting cells named in the lecture?

- [x] Natural killer cell
- [ ] Dendritic cell
- [ ] Macrophage
- [ ] Neutrophil

> The antigen-presenting cells named in the lecture are dendritic cells, macrophages and neutrophils, phagocytes that digest a pathogen and display its fragments on MHC. Natural killer cells kill virus-infected or cancerous cells but do not present antigen. See [[T Cells and Antigen Presentation#1. T cells need an introduction]].

## The Fc (crystallisable) fragment of an antibody

- [ ] is formed by the variable regions of the heavy and light chains.
- [ ] binds antigen with high specificity.
- [ ] is found at the tips of the two arms of the Y.
- [x] binds to receptors on immune cells to trigger their activation.

> The constant regions of the heavy chains give rise to the Fc stem, which binds to receptors on immune cells to trigger their activation; the variable regions give rise to the two Fab arms, which bind antigen with high specificity. See [[B Cells and Antibodies#2. The shape of an antibody]].

## Compared with the primary antibody response, the secondary response

- [ ] takes 5 to 10 days to appear and is mainly IgM.
- [ ] takes 1 to 3 days to appear and is mainly IgM.
- [x] takes 1 to 3 days to appear and is mainly IgG.
- [ ] takes 5 to 10 days to appear and is mainly IgG.

> The memory B cells left by the primary response make the secondary response more rapid (1 to 3 days instead of 5 to 10), much larger (a higher antibody titre), mainly IgG rather than IgM, and more specific through affinity maturation. See [[Immunological Memory – Primary and Secondary Responses#3. The secondary response]].
