Ten multiple-choice questions on the fifth pulmonary lecture: the nose, the particles it filters, cilia, mucus, lysozyme and lactoferrin, pattern recognition receptors, TLR4, the neutrophil and the definition of ARDS. Written in the style of the lecturer's example questions. Each answer comes with a short explanation and the note and section that cover it.

## The highest velocity of air movement in the respiratory tract is at the

- [ ] trachea
- [x] nasal valve
- [ ] terminal bronchioles
- [ ] alveoli

> The nasal valve, 1.5 cm in from the nostril, carries air at 18 m/s (0.15–0.25 L/s), which throws particles against the mucosa. Velocity falls steadily down the tract as total cross-sectional area grows, and in the alveoli gas moves by diffusion. See [[The Lung's First Line of Defence – Nose, Cilia, Mucus and Antimicrobial Peptides#2. The nose a filter that sorts particles by size]].

## The particles that the nasal mucosa filters efficiently are mainly

- [ ] smaller than 0.5 µm
- [ ] 10–100 µm
- [ ] about 0.001 µm
- [x] 0.5–3 µm

> Particles of 0.5–3 µm are caught by the nasal mucosa and carried by the cilia to the nasopharynx, where most are eventually swallowed; filtration of particles below 0.5 µm is low. How far a particle penetrates depends on its diameter, density, time in the tract and velocity. See [[The Lung's First Line of Defence – Nose, Cilia, Mucus and Antimicrobial Peptides#2. The nose a filter that sorts particles by size]].

## Cilia are powered by

- [x] dynein motor proteins using ATP
- [ ] kinesin
- [ ] actin and myosin
- [ ] flagellin

> Each respiratory cell carries 200–300 cilia that beat with the energy of ATP hydrolysis by dynein, moving mucus and trapped matter up and out of the airway; they resemble but are unrelated to prokaryotic flagella. See [[The Lung's First Line of Defence – Nose, Cilia, Mucus and Antimicrobial Peptides#3. Cilia the escalator that carries mucus out of the airway]].

## Mucus is secreted by

- [ ] type II alveolar cells
- [ ] alveolar macrophages
- [x] goblet cells
- [ ] neutrophils

> Goblet cells secrete the mucus, 95–97% water with mucins, salts, innate immune proteins and lipids, which acts as a physical barrier and lubricant, supports cell function and destroys and removes pathogens. Type II cells make surfactant. See [[The Lung's First Line of Defence – Nose, Cilia, Mucus and Antimicrobial Peptides#4. Mucus the sticky blanket that traps and kills microbes]].

## Lysozyme

- [ ] sequesters iron
- [x] hydrolyses the (1→4)-β-linkages of peptidoglycan
- [ ] forms pores in bacterial membranes
- [ ] is a cytokine

> Lysozyme breaks the bonds between N-acetylmuramic acid and N-acetyl-D-glucosamine in the bacterial cell wall (and in chitodextrins). Sequestering iron is lactoferrin, and pore formation is the antimicrobial peptides. See [[The Lung's First Line of Defence – Nose, Cilia, Mucus and Antimicrobial Peptides#5. Molecules that attack microbes antimicrobial peptides, lysozyme and lactoferrin]].

## Lactoferrin protects the airway by

- [ ] disrupting bacterial membranes
- [ ] activating complement
- [ ] digesting peptidoglycan
- [x] sequestering iron

> By binding the iron that bacteria need to grow, lactoferrin starves them; hepcidin, mostly from the liver, is the other iron-chelating peptide. See [[The Lung's First Line of Defence – Nose, Cilia, Mucus and Antimicrobial Peptides#5. Molecules that attack microbes antimicrobial peptides, lysozyme and lactoferrin]].

## Pattern recognition receptors (PRRs)

- [x] are encoded in the germline, with a set specificity and about 100 different kinds
- [ ] are generated by somatic recombination
- [ ] differ between clones of the same cell type
- [ ] are found only on B cells

> Innate cells carry about 100 PRRs, such as Toll-like receptors and NOD-like receptors, each with a fixed specificity for a pathogen-associated molecular pattern shared by whole classes of microbes, as Janeway proposed in 1989. Clonal variation is the hallmark of adaptive receptors. See [[Lung Immune Cells – Toll-like Receptors, Macrophages and Neutrophils#2. How innate cells recognise a microbe]].

## Toll-like receptor 4 recognises

- [ ] flagellin
- [ ] double-stranded RNA
- [x] lipopolysaccharide
- [ ] CpG DNA

> TLR4, with MD2, binds the LPS of Gram-negative bacteria such as E. coli, Pseudomonas, Klebsiella, Legionella and Haemophilus, and also the DAMPs HMGB1 and S100A8. Flagellin is TLR5, dsRNA is TLR3 and CpG DNA is TLR9. See [[Lung Immune Cells – Toll-like Receptors, Macrophages and Neutrophils#3. Toll-like receptors structure and signal]].

## The most common white blood cell is the

- [ ] lymphocyte
- [x] neutrophil
- [ ] monocyte
- [ ] eosinophil

> Neutrophils make up to 70% of white cells (2,500–7,000 per µL), circulate in an inactive state, live only hours to days and are constantly replenished from the bone marrow; their total number is a critical indicator of health. See [[Lung Immune Cells – Toll-like Receptors, Macrophages and Neutrophils#5. The neutrophil]].

## The Berlin definition of ARDS requires a PaO2:FiO2 ratio of

- [ ] 500 mmHg or more
- [ ] 760 mmHg or less
- [ ] 100 mmHg or less
- [x] 300 mmHg or less

> ARDS is diagnosed by acute onset, a PaO2 to FiO2 ratio of 300 mmHg or less, and bilateral infiltrates on chest X-ray consistent with pulmonary oedema. In Europe it affects 3–6 per 100,000 person-years. See [[Lung Disease and Exercise – ARDS, Asthma and COVID#1. When the alveoli flood ARDS]].
