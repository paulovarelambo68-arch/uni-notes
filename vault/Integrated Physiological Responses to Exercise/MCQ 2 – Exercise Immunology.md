Eight more multiple-choice questions on Exercise Immunology, covering the treatment of acute inflammation, intense training and infection, the acute effects of exercise on leukocytes and their trafficking, IL-6 and the effects of chronic exercise at moderate and high intensity. Each answer comes with a short explanation and the note that covers it.

## Cryotherapy (ice) applied within 24 to 48 hours of an acute injury reduces secondary ischaemic injury because it

- [ ] blocks the production of prostaglandins.
- [ ] increases blood flow and the infiltration of immune cells.
- [x] reduces metabolism and oxidative stress in the tissue.
- [ ] stimulates the release of inflammatory mediators.

> Ice causes vasoconstriction, reduces the transmission of sensory pain signals and reduces metabolism and oxidative stress, which limits secondary ischaemic injury. Blocking prostaglandin production is the mechanism of NSAIDs such as ibuprofen, used short term, topically or orally. See [[Inflammation – Acute, Chronic and CRP#3. Helpful or harmful to treat or not]].

## Regarding intense training and infection, which of the following statements is INCORRECT?

- [ ] Athletes show a high incidence of URTI during intense training, even for a short time.
- [x] Infections account for the great majority of upper respiratory symptoms in elite athletes.
- [ ] A dose-response relationship has been found between yearly training distance and URTI risk in long-distance runners.
- [ ] Not all studies support the J-shaped curve.

> Only about one-third of the upper respiratory symptoms of elite athletes are infections (about 5% bacterial, the rest viral); another third are identifiable non-infectious inflammatory diseases and a third are of unknown cause. URTI incidence above 40% was seen in swimmers, hockey and squash players in intense training, and the J-shaped curve remains debated. See [[Exercise and Infection Risk – J-Shaped Curve and Open Window#4. The evidence for the right arm intense training]] and [[Exercise and Infection Risk – J-Shaped Curve and Open Window#6. Infection or inflammation?]].

## After a bout of intense, long-duration exercise, which of the following is seen in the blood?

- [ ] Neutrophils fall to low levels while lymphocytes and NK cells rise.
- [ ] Both neutrophils and lymphocytes fall below their resting levels.
- [ ] Both neutrophils and lymphocytes stay elevated for the following 72 hours or more.
- [x] Neutrophils stay elevated while lymphocytes and NK cells fall to low levels.

> Neutrophil concentration increases during and after exercise, whereas lymphocytes and NK cells increase during exercise but fall to low levels afterwards, the dip that is the open window, most pronounced 2 to 6 hours post-exercise. The Pedersen graph puts the window at no longer than 24 hours and the open window hypothesis at 3 to 72 hours. See [[Mechanisms of Exercise-Induced Immune Changes#3. What the cell counts do during and after a hard bout]].

## Lymphocytes express high numbers of β2-adrenergic receptors. Activation of these receptors during exercise results in

- [x] increased expression of cell adhesion molecules.
- [ ] increased release of cortisol from the adrenal cortex.
- [ ] decreased secretion of IgA into the saliva.
- [ ] increased release of IL-6 from skeletal muscle.

> β2-adrenergic receptors are upregulated with exercise, and their activation by adrenaline and noradrenaline increases the expression of cell adhesion molecules, which pair with partners on endothelial cells to take lymphocytes through rolling, adhesion and diapedesis across the vessel wall. See [[Mechanisms of Exercise-Induced Immune Changes#4. Where the cells come from and how they cross the wall]].

## Chronically elevated circulating IL-6 is associated with

- [ ] improved insulin secretion and glycaemic control.
- [ ] enhanced anti-tumour activity of natural killer cells in tumours.
- [x] cardiometabolic disease, autoimmunity and cancer progression.
- [ ] reduced all-cause mortality.

> This is the IL-6 paradox: the transient IL-6 of acute exercise is anti-inflammatory and metabolically beneficial, but chronic elevation, from sustained release by inflamed tissues such as visceral fat, is associated with cardiometabolic disease, heightened autoimmunity and inflammation, cancer progression and increased all-cause mortality. See [[IL-6 in Exercise#5. The IL-6 paradox]].

## Chronic exercise at moderate intensity with adequate recovery produces which of the following over time?

- [x] Lower baseline inflammation, shown by a lower CRP.
- [ ] An increase in the number of blood leukocytes measured at rest.
- [ ] A decrease in salivary IgA.
- [ ] Increased baseline pro-inflammatory cytokines.

> Repeated mobilisation and redistribution of immune cells produces an unchanged blood leukocyte number, more efficient trafficking, lower baseline inflammation (lower CRP), improved innate and adaptive regulation, more T cells including Tregs, lower pro-inflammatory and higher anti-inflammatory cytokines, slower immunosenescence and maintained or increased salivary IgA. See [[Chronic Exercise and Immune Function – Moderate versus High Intensity#2. Chronic moderate exercise with adequate recovery]].

## In chronic high-intensity exercise, the decrease in natural killer cell activity is caused by

- [ ] impaired phagocytosis and a reduced oxidative burst.
- [ ] desensitised signalling.
- [ ] persistent muscle and tissue stress.
- [x] chronic catecholamine and cortisol exposure.

> Chronic exposure to catecholamines and cortisol reduces NK cell activity, lowering early antiviral defence. Impaired phagocytosis and oxidative burst reduce neutrophil function, desensitised signalling lowers monocyte/macrophage responsiveness, and persistent tissue stress raises IL-6 and TNF-α, giving low-grade systemic inflammation. See [[Chronic Exercise and Immune Function – Moderate versus High Intensity#3. Chronic high-intensity exercise]].

## In high-intensity exercise, which of the following has been shown to increase IL-6 secretion?

- [ ] Cortisol
- [x] Lactate
- [ ] IL-10
- [ ] Glucose

> Lactate generated during strenuous cycling tracked closely with a roughly 5-fold rise in IL-6, and in adults with obesity cycling at 80% VO2peak the rise in IL-6 correlated with the rise in lactate. Whether lactate is involved in IL-6 release at lower intensities is unknown. IL-10 is one of the anti-inflammatory cytokines that IL-6 itself stimulates. See [[IL-6 in Exercise#1. Two sources, two faces]] and [[IL-6 in Exercise#4. Immune effects of exercise-induced IL-6]].
