A second set of eight multiple-choice questions drawn from all three lectures, Immunology I, Immunology II and Exercise Immunology. Each answer comes with a short explanation and the note and section that cover it.

## Which of the following innate immune cells is a lymphocyte?

- [ ] Neutrophil
- [ ] Dendritic cell
- [ ] Mast cell
- [x] Natural killer cell

> NK cells are lymphocytes (10 to 15% of blood lymphocytes) that nevertheless belong to the innate branch, because they act at once without prior exposure. Only B and T lymphocytes are adaptive cells, and neutrophils, dendritic cells and mast cells arise mainly from the myeloid family. See [[Cells and tissues of the immune system#5. The cast by branch]].

## Acute inflammation has a rapid onset, usually resolves within a few days and is characterised by the infiltration of

- [x] neutrophils.
- [ ] lymphocytes.
- [ ] plasma cells.
- [ ] regulatory T cells.

> Acute inflammation is mainly an innate immune response, and neutrophils, the most abundant white cell and the first to arrive at an infection, are the cells that infiltrate. B and T lymphocytes, plasma cells and Tregs belong to the adaptive branch, which joins in chronic inflammation. See [[Inflammation – Acute, Chronic and CRP#2. The acute inflammatory event, step by step]] and [[Cells and tissues of the immune system#5. The cast by branch]].

## C3a, C4a and C5a are

- [ ] opsonins that coat the surface of pathogens for phagocytosis.
- [ ] the terminal complement components that assemble into the membrane attack complex.
- [x] mediators of inflammation that bind receptors on immune and endothelial cells.
- [ ] cytokines released by macrophages to recruit neutrophils.

> The small fragments C3a, C4a and C5a mediate inflammation by binding to receptors on neutrophils, macrophages, mast cells, basophils and endothelial cells. The large fragments C3b and C4b are the opsonins, and C5b to C9 form the membrane attack complex. See [[Complement and Natural Killer Cells#3. The three functions]].

## An antibody molecule is made up of

- [ ] four identical polypeptide chains.
- [x] two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains.
- [ ] one heavy chain and three light chains.
- [ ] two heavy chains and two light chains, all with different variable regions.

> The four chains are two identical heavy and two identical light chains, each with a variable region at the tip, giving the two identical Fab antigen-binding arms, and a constant region; the heavy chains’ constant regions form the Fc stem. See [[B Cells and Antibodies#2. The shape of an antibody]].

## Which of the following cell types does NOT undergo clonal selection?

- [ ] B cell
- [ ] Helper T cell
- [ ] Cytotoxic T cell
- [x] Dendritic cell

> Clonal selection occurs for both T and B cells, the only cells with receptors for a single antigen. Dendritic cells are innate antigen-presenting cells: they present antigen to T cells, which then undergo clonal selection, but they do not undergo it themselves. See [[Adaptive Immunity – Principles and Clonal Selection#3. Clonal selection]].

## The ‘open window’ of reduced immune function after exhaustive exercise

- [x] lasts 3 to 72 hours, with the most pronounced decrease 2 to 6 hours post-exercise.
- [ ] begins about three days after the exercise and lasts for about a week.
- [ ] is most pronounced during the exercise itself, while the immune cells are being mobilised.
- [ ] occurs only after moderate exercise and never after exhaustive exercise.

> Prolonged exhaustive activity reduces the immune response for 3 to 72 hours, with the fall in lymphocytes and NK cells most pronounced 2 to 6 hours post-exercise and, by cell counts, over within 24 hours. During the exercise itself these cells rise; the window opens when they drop afterwards. See [[Exercise and Infection Risk – J-Shaped Curve and Open Window#5. The open window hypothesis]] and [[Mechanisms of Exercise-Induced Immune Changes#3. What the cell counts do during and after a hard bout]].

## Which of the following is a consequence of chronic high-intensity exercise without adequate recovery?

- [ ] Increased natural killer cell activity and improved early antiviral defence
- [ ] Slower age-related immune decline, with less immunosenescence
- [x] Impaired neutrophil phagocytosis, leading to slower bacterial clearance
- [ ] Maintained or increased salivary IgA with lower baseline CRP

> Chronic high-intensity training impairs neutrophil phagocytosis and oxidative burst (slower bacterial clearance), lowers NK activity and monocyte/macrophage responsiveness, and raises IL-6 and TNF-α, giving low-grade systemic inflammation. The lecture’s summary adds elevated stress hormones, reduced antibody responses and more URTIs. The other three are adaptations to moderate training. See [[Chronic Exercise and Immune Function – Moderate versus High Intensity#3. Chronic high-intensity exercise]] and [[Chronic Exercise and Immune Function – Moderate versus High Intensity#5. The whole picture]].

## Regarding athletes in heavy training, which of the following statements is correct?

- [ ] They typically present with the serious illnesses associated with immunodeficiency.
- [x] They mainly show an increased incidence of upper respiratory tract infections.
- [ ] They show no measurable change in any immune parameter.
- [ ] They are protected from infection by chronically elevated IL-6.

> Athletes do not present with the serious illnesses of immunodeficiency, only URTIs, but repeated training before full recovery produces small changes in several parameters that may add up to chronic immunosuppression, which is why intensity must be balanced with rest, nutrition and recovery. See [[Chronic Exercise and Immune Function – Moderate versus High Intensity#4. Are athletes immunocompromised?]].
