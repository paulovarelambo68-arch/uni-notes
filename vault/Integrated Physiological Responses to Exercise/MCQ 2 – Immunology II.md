Eight more multiple-choice questions on Immunology II, covering clonal selection, antigen presentation on MHC, cytotoxic T cells, B cell receptors, the functions and isotypes of antibodies and immunological memory. Each answer comes with a short explanation and the note and section that cover it.

## In clonal selection, the role of the antigen is to

- [ ] instruct a lymphocyte to build a receptor that fits it.
- [ ] cause every lymphocyte it meets to divide rapidly.
- [x] select the few pre-existing lymphocytes whose receptors fit it.
- [ ] display itself on MHC so that lymphocytes are able to recognise it.

> Lymphocytes carry their single-antigen receptors before any antigen is met, so the antigen selects, out of an extremely diverse repertoire, the rare cells whose receptor fits; only those bind, become activated and divide into a clone of effector and memory cells (Burnet, 1957). Display on MHC is the job of antigen-presenting cells, not of the antigen itself. See [[Adaptive Immunity – Principles and Clonal Selection#3. Clonal selection]].

## Highly specific T cell receptors recognise antigen that is

- [ ] free in the plasma.
- [ ] bound to a B cell receptor.
- [ ] coated with complement proteins and antibodies.
- [x] presented on MHC by an antigen-presenting cell.

> A T cell receptor cannot recognise a free microbe, so T cells need to be ‘introduced’ to antigen by an APC, which phagocytoses the pathogen and uses the Major Histocompatibility Complex to display fragments of the digested antigen on its surface. This is how the innate branch instructs the adaptive branch. See [[T Cells and Antigen Presentation#1. T cells need an introduction]].

## Regarding cytotoxic (killer) T cells, which of the following statements is INCORRECT?

- [ ] They kill foreign cells or infected host cells by direct cell-to-cell contact.
- [x] They recognise their targets without antigen having to be presented to them.
- [ ] They release perforin and granzyme.
- [ ] They can attack body cells that have become cancerous.

> Like all T cells, a cytotoxic T cell is specific for one antigen and must first be presented with it on MHC; it is natural killer cells that need no prior presentation. Both kill with perforin and granzyme, and the lecture’s micrograph shows a killer T cell attacking a cancer cell. See [[T Cells and Antigen Presentation#2. Three kinds of T cell]].

## Regarding B cells, which of the following statements is correct?

- [x] B cell receptors are antibodies.
- [ ] B cells carry out cell-mediated immunity.
- [ ] B cells mature in the thymus.
- [ ] B cells kill infected host cells by releasing perforin and granzyme.

> The receptor a B cell uses to catch antigen is a membrane-bound antibody, so after activation it produces antibodies with the same specificity as its BCR. Cell-mediated immunity, maturation in the thymus and killing with perforin and granzyme all describe T cells. See [[B Cells and Antibodies#1. B cell receptors are antibodies]].

## Which function of antibodies is achieved by direct binding alone, without the involvement of other immune cells or complement?

- [x] Neutralisation of toxins and microbes
- [ ] Opsonisation and phagocytosis of microbes
- [ ] Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity
- [ ] Lysis of microbes

> Neutralisation needs only the Fab arms, which block a toxin or microbe by binding it. Opsonisation for phagocytosis and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity depend on phagocytes or NK cells gripping the Fc stem, and lysis follows activation of complement. See [[B Cells and Antibodies#3. What antibodies do]].

## Which antibody isotype is a pentamer, made up of five Ig units?

- [ ] IgG
- [ ] IgA
- [ ] IgD
- [x] IgM

> IgM has five Ig units joined by a joining chain, IgA often has two (a dimer) and IgG, IgE and IgD each have a single Y unit. The five isotypes come from the five types of heavy chain (γ, μ, α, δ, ε). See [[B Cells and Antibodies#4. The five isotypes]].

## Regarding IgG, which of the following statements is INCORRECT?

- [ ] It comprises 70 to 75% of circulating immunoglobulins.
- [ ] It has four subclasses.
- [x] It is the first antibody produced in response to a new infection.
- [ ] It is the only immunoglobulin to be transported across the placenta.

> IgM is the first antibody produced in response to a new infection and dominates the primary response; IgG dominates the secondary response. The other three statements are the lecture’s facts about IgG. See [[B Cells and Antibodies#4. The five isotypes]].

## A person is immunised with antigen X. Weeks later they are given antigen X again together with a new antigen, Y. What antibody responses would be expected?

- [ ] A rapid, large response to both X and Y.
- [x] A rapid, large response to X and a slow, small response to Y.
- [ ] A slow, small response to both X and Y.
- [ ] A rapid, large response to Y and a slow, small response to X.

> Memory is specific to the antigen already met: memory anti-X B cells mount a secondary response to X, whereas Y, never seen before, gets only a primary response from naive anti-Y cells. This is the lecture’s immunologic memory figure and the principle behind vaccination. See [[Immunological Memory – Primary and Secondary Responses#4. Reading the memory figure]].
