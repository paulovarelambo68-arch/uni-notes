Ten more multiple-choice questions on the first pulmonary lecture: lung size, the alveolar pores, Boyle's law, residual volume, the reserve volumes, obstructive and restrictive patterns, alveolar ventilation, minute ventilation in athletes, shunt and surfactant. Each answer comes with a short explanation and the note and section that cover it.

## The gas-exchange surface of the lungs is approximately

- [ ] 7–10 m2
- [x] 70–100 m2
- [ ] 700–1000 m2
- [ ] 4–6 m2

> The alveoli spread the exchange surface over 70–100 m2. 4–6 L is the volume of the lungs, which weigh about 1 kg and are only 10% solid tissue, the rest being air and blood. See [[The Lungs and the Breathing Pump – Alveoli, Airways, Muscles, Surfactant and the Valsalva Manoeuvre#1. What the lungs are for]].

## The pores between adjacent alveoli

- [ ] are where gas exchange with the capillary blood takes place
- [ ] close when surfactant is released
- [x] allow surfactant to spread and provide collateral ventilation
- [ ] are present from birth

> The pores let surfactant disperse for easier inflation, allow collateral ventilation and so prevent collapse, and are fully developed only at 3–4 years of age. Gas exchange happens across the alveolar-capillary membrane, not through the pores. See [[The Lungs and the Breathing Pump – Alveoli, Airways, Muscles, Surfactant and the Valsalva Manoeuvre#2. The alveoli]].

## The fall in intrapulmonary pressure below atmospheric pressure when the thoracic cavity enlarges is explained by

- [ ] Dalton's law
- [ ] Henry's law
- [ ] Starling's law
- [x] Boyle's law

> Boyle's law says that the pressure of a gas falls as its volume rises, so enlarging the chest lowers the pressure of the air in the lungs and air flows in down the gradient until the two pressures are equal and inspiration is complete. Dalton's law is about partial pressures, Henry's about dissolved gas and Starling's about the heart. See [[The Lungs and the Breathing Pump – Alveoli, Airways, Muscles, Surfactant and the Valsalva Manoeuvre#5. Breathing in]].

## Residual lung volume increases with age because of

- [x] loss of elastic tissue (fibrosis)
- [ ] growth of new alveoli
- [ ] stronger expiratory muscles
- [ ] increased surfactant production

> The lungs lose elastic recoil with age, so less air can be forced out and more remains after a maximal exhalation (0.8–1.2 L in healthy young women, 0.9–1.4 L in young men). Residual volume is the air that keeps gas exchange going between breaths and prevents swings in blood gases. See [[Lung Volumes and Capacities – Spirometry, FEV1 and FVC#2. The air that never leaves residual volume]].

## The inspiratory reserve volume is approximately

- [ ] 1.2 L
- [x] 3.0 L
- [ ] 4.8 L
- [ ] 6.0 L

> IRV, the deepest extra inhalation after a normal breath, is about 3.0 L; 1.2 L is the expiratory reserve volume, 4.8 L the average male FVC and 6.0 L the average male total lung capacity. See [[Lung Volumes and Capacities – Spirometry, FEV1 and FVC#1. Measuring a breath the spirogram]].

## An FEV1/FVC ratio of 70% or less indicates

- [ ] restrictive lung disease
- [ ] normal lung function
- [ ] increased expiratory power
- [x] airway obstruction

> Obstruction (COPD, asthma, bronchiectasis, cystic fibrosis) slows exhalation, so a smaller share of the FVC comes out in the first second, 42% in the asthma example, and it becomes hard to exhale before the next breath is needed. Restrictive disease such as pulmonary fibrosis limits inhalation instead: the FVC is small but the ratio stays high (90% in the example). See [[Lung Volumes and Capacities – Spirometry, FEV1 and FVC#3. How fast, not just how much dynamic volumes and FEV1]].

## Of a 500 mL tidal breath at rest, the volume that reaches the alveoli and takes part in gas exchange is about

- [x] 350 mL
- [ ] 150 mL
- [ ] 500 mL
- [ ] 250 mL

> About 150 mL fills the anatomic dead space, leaving about 350 mL to mix with the alveolar air. This alveolar ventilation sets the gas concentrations at the alveolar-capillary membrane and is the more functionally relevant measure, but it is difficult to measure directly. 250 mL is the oxygen taken up per minute at rest. See [[Minute and Alveolar Ventilation – Dead Space, the Ventilation–Perfusion Ratio and Shunt#4. Dead space and alveolar ventilation]].

## In strenuous exercise a male endurance athlete's minute ventilation can reach about

- [ ] 6 L/min
- [ ] 60 L/min
- [x] 160 L/min
- [ ] 600 L/min

> Healthy adults reach about 100 L/min (35–45 breaths/min x 2.0 L), male endurance athletes about 160 L/min, and some exceed 200 L/min, through both faster and deeper breathing. 6 L/min is the resting value. See [[Minute and Alveolar Ventilation – Dead Space, the Ventilation–Perfusion Ratio and Shunt#2. Minute ventilation and its numbers]].

## A pulmonary shunt

- [ ] raises arterial PO2
- [x] lowers arterial PO2 and widens the alveolar-arterial O2 gradient
- [ ] raises alveolar PCO2
- [ ] raises the ventilation–perfusion ratio

> Shunted blood passes from the right side of the heart to the left without taking part in gas exchange, for example past unventilated alveoli, through vessels opened by the high flow of exercise, or through a congenital heart defect. It dilutes the arterial blood, so PaO2 falls while alveolar PO2 is unchanged and the A-a gradient grows; it can be life-threatening in a patient who needs air or oxygen support. See [[Minute and Alveolar Ventilation – Dead Space, the Ventilation–Perfusion Ratio and Shunt#6. Blood that misses the air pulmonary shunt]].

## Surfactant

- [ ] increases the surface tension of the alveolar lining
- [ ] decreases lung compliance
- [ ] thickens the alveolar membrane
- [x] reduces surface tension and so increases lung compliance

> By interrupting the water layer lining the alveoli, surfactant lowers surface tension, which makes the lungs easier to inflate (higher compliance), reduces the energy needed for inflation and deflation, prevents the collapse of small alveoli (atelectasis) and prevents oedema. See [[The Lungs and the Breathing Pump – Alveoli, Airways, Muscles, Surfactant and the Valsalva Manoeuvre#7. Surfactant and compliance]].
