Ten more multiple-choice questions on lung immunity and disease: the water content of mucus, the diseases of the cilia, the defensins, hepcidin, the TLR structure, PAMPs and DAMPs, M1 macrophages, the oxidative burst and asthma in Ireland. Each answer comes with a short explanation and the note and section that cover it.

## Mucus is what percentage water?

- [ ] 50–60%
- [ ] 75–80%
- [x] 95–97%
- [ ] 100%

> Almost all water, thickened by mucins and carrying salts, innate immune proteins and lipids. See [[The Lung's First Line of Defence – Nose, Cilia, Mucus and Antimicrobial Peptides#4. Mucus the sticky blanket that traps and kills microbes]].

## Cilia are dysfunctional in

- [x] cystic fibrosis and primary ciliary dyskinesia
- [ ] asthma and COPD
- [ ] ARDS
- [ ] pneumonia

> In both diseases the mucociliary escalator fails and matter is not cleared upwards; cadmium, copper and magnesium can also interfere with ciliary function, and smoking reduces ciliary motility. See [[The Lung's First Line of Defence – Nose, Cilia, Mucus and Antimicrobial Peptides#3. Cilia the escalator that carries mucus out of the airway]].

## α-defensins are produced by

- [ ] epithelial cells
- [ ] the liver
- [ ] goblet cells
- [x] neutrophils and NK cells

> Defensins are very small (18–45 amino acids) membrane-disrupting peptides: α-defensins come from neutrophils and NK cells, β-defensins from leukocytes and epithelial cells, and both can be secreted or act in the phagosome. See [[The Lung's First Line of Defence – Nose, Cilia, Mucus and Antimicrobial Peptides#5. Molecules that attack microbes antimicrobial peptides, lysozyme and lactoferrin]].

## Hepcidin is

- [ ] a pore-forming peptide from neutrophils
- [x] an iron-chelating peptide produced mostly by the liver
- [ ] an enzyme that digests peptidoglycan
- [ ] a cytokine

> Hepcidin (84 amino acids, processed to smaller forms) chelates iron; it is strong against E. coli and Neisseria cinerea, weak against Staphylococcus aureus and Candida albicans and inactive against Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Pore-forming is cathelicidin (LL37), 12–80 amino acids. See [[The Lung's First Line of Defence – Nose, Cilia, Mucus and Antimicrobial Peptides#5. Molecules that attack microbes antimicrobial peptides, lysozyme and lactoferrin]].

## The cytosolic domain of a Toll-like receptor is the

- [ ] leucine-rich repeat
- [ ] transmembrane helix
- [x] TIR (toll/interleukin-1 receptor) domain
- [ ] MD2 co-receptor

> A TLR has an extracellular horseshoe of 18–25 leucine-rich repeats that binds the PAMP, a transmembrane helix and a cytosolic TIR domain; on binding, the receptors usually dimerise and TIR starts the signalling cascade (MyD88, TRIF, NF-κB) that leads to phagocytosis, cytokines and co-stimulatory molecules. See [[Lung Immune Cells – Toll-like Receptors, Macrophages and Neutrophils#3. Toll-like receptors structure and signal]].

## Peptidoglycan and lipopolysaccharide are examples of

- [x] pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs)
- [ ] pattern recognition receptors
- [ ] damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)
- [ ] cytokines

> More than 1,000 PAMPs exist; they are structures shared by classes of microbes, usually essential for the microbe's survival and absent from host cells. Peptidoglycan and teichoic acid mark Gram-positive bacteria (S. aureus, S. pneumoniae), LPS, porins and lipoproteins the Gram-negative outer membrane (E. coli, P. aeruginosa). See [[Lung Immune Cells – Toll-like Receptors, Macrophages and Neutrophils#2. How innate cells recognise a microbe]].

## HMGB1 and S100A8, which TLR4 also recognises, are

- [ ] PAMPs
- [ ] forms of LPS
- [ ] cytokines
- [x] DAMPs

> Damage-associated molecular patterns are host molecules released by injured cells, so TLR4 answers both infection (LPS) and tissue damage. See [[Lung Immune Cells – Toll-like Receptors, Macrophages and Neutrophils#3. Toll-like receptors structure and signal]].

## M1 macrophages are

- [ ] broadly anti-inflammatory
- [x] broadly pro-inflammatory, induced by IFN-γ and LPS
- [ ] induced by IL-4 and IL-13
- [ ] marked by CD163 and CD206

> M1 cells are driven by IFN-γ, LPS, TNF-α and GM-CSF through STAT1, IRF1, IRF5 and NF-κB, secrete IFN-γ, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8 and TNF-α, make iNOS and ROS, carry CD16, CD32, CD64, CD80/86 and HLA-DR, and phagocytose more. IL-4, IL-13, IL-10 and TGF-β induce the anti-inflammatory M2 cells, marked by CD163 and CD206. See [[Lung Immune Cells – Toll-like Receptors, Macrophages and Neutrophils#4. The macrophage two forms]].

## The neutrophil oxidative burst is mediated by

- [ ] lysozyme
- [ ] carbonic anhydrase
- [x] NADPH oxidase (NOX2)
- [ ] dynein

> NOX2 converts O2 and NADPH into superoxide, which with hydrogen peroxide and hypochlorous acid oxidises pathogens during phagocytosis (2O2 + NADPH → 2O2− + NADP+ + H+); the burst is a test of neutrophil health, and released ROS also let the cell move through the matrix but can damage tissue. See [[Lung Immune Cells – Toll-like Receptors, Macrophages and Neutrophils#6. The oxidative burst]].

## Asthma affects what proportion of Irish children?

- [x] 25%
- [ ] 7%
- [ ] 50%
- [ ] 2%

> 25% of Irish children and 7% of adults, and up to half of cases are not controlled; the causative allergens are house dust mite, pollen, animal dander and mould spores. See [[Lung Disease and Exercise – ARDS, Asthma and COVID#2. When the airways narrow asthma]].
