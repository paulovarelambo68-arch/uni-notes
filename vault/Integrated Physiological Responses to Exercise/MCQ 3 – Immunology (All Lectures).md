A third set of eight multiple-choice questions drawn from all three lectures, Immunology I, Immunology II and Exercise Immunology. Each answer comes with a short explanation and the note and section that cover it.

## A cytokine that acts on the same cell that secreted it is said to act in a(n) __________ manner.

- [x] autocrine
- [ ] paracrine
- [ ] endocrine
- [ ] exocrine

> Cytokines act on the cell that made them (autocrine) or on nearby cells (paracrine), always through specific receptors; the loops back to the same cell in the cytokine network figure are autocrine signals. See [[Cytokines#1. What a cytokine is]] and [[Cytokines#3. The cytokine network]].

## Which of the following cells belongs to the adaptive rather than the innate branch of the immune system?

- [ ] Natural killer cell
- [ ] Dendritic cell
- [ ] Eosinophil
- [x] B lymphocyte

> Only B and T lymphocytes are adaptive cells. Monocytes/macrophages, neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, mast cells, dendritic cells and NK cells are all innate, even though NK cells are lymphocytes and dendritic cells instruct the T cells. See [[Cells and tissues of the immune system#5. The cast by branch]].

## The membrane attack complex kills a pathogen by

- [ ] coating it so that phagocytes can engulf it.
- [x] forming a pore that disrupts fluid balance, leading to lysis.
- [ ] delivering granzyme through a pore, which then induces apoptosis.
- [ ] releasing histamine and prostaglandins.

> The terminal pathway assembles C5b, C6, C7, C8 and several C9 molecules into a pore through the pathogen’s membrane; water and ions rush in, disrupting fluid balance until the cell lyses. Granzyme and apoptosis belong to NK and cytotoxic T cells, and coating is opsonisation by C3b. See [[Complement and Natural Killer Cells#3. The three functions]].

## Which two antibody isotypes are expressed together on the surface membrane of many B cells, where they act as B cell receptors?

- [ ] IgG and IgA
- [ ] IgA and IgE
- [x] IgM and IgD
- [ ] IgG and IgM

> Monomeric IgM and IgD sit on the B cell surface as the B cell receptor. IgG (70 to 75% of circulating Ig, the only one to cross the placenta) and IgA (secretions such as saliva) are the main secreted antibodies, and IgE features in allergy and defence against helminths. See [[B Cells and Antibodies#4. The five isotypes]].

## Which of the following is NOT a function of antibodies?

- [ ] Neutralisation of toxins and microbes by direct binding
- [x] Engulfing and digesting microbes
- [ ] Activation of complement
- [ ] Targeting of microbes to immune cells for destruction

> Antibodies cannot phagocytose anything: they target microbes to immune cells for destruction, coating them (opsonisation) so that phagocytes grip the Fc stem and engulf them, or coating an infected cell so that NK cells kill it (antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity). Neutralisation and complement activation are their other two functions. See [[B Cells and Antibodies#3. What antibodies do]].

## Among elite athletes with upper respiratory symptoms, which of the following is correct?

- [ ] Almost all cases are caused by viral infections.
- [ ] Almost all cases are non-infectious inflammatory diseases.
- [ ] About one-third are infections, and most of these are bacterial.
- [x] About one-third are infections, and most of these are viral.

> Only about a third of cases are infections, mostly viral, with bacteria behind about 5% of cases; another third are identifiable non-infectious inflammatory diseases and the last third are of unknown cause. This is why the lecture asks ‘infection or inflammation?’ of the J-shaped curve data. See [[Exercise and Infection Risk – J-Shaped Curve and Open Window#6. Infection or inflammation?]].

## Which of the following is a reason why acute inflammation after an injury is considered helpful?

- [x] Pain and loss of function protect the tissue from further damage.
- [ ] Vasoconstriction in the injured area limits swelling.
- [ ] The production of prostaglandins in the tissue is blocked.
- [ ] Neutrophils are prevented from entering the injured tissue.

> Pain and loss of function protect against further damage, the increased blood flow and infiltration of immune cells protect against microbial infection, and some mediators promote wound healing and tissue regeneration. Vasoconstriction and blocked prostaglandins are the effects of treatments (ice and NSAIDs), not of inflammation itself. See [[Inflammation – Acute, Chronic and CRP#3. Helpful or harmful to treat or not]].

## Regarding IL-6, which of the following statements is correct?

- [ ] The IL-6 gene is continuously active in resting muscle and is switched off as soon as the exercise session begins.
- [ ] IL-6 released by muscle in acute exercise increases TNF-α production by immune cells.
- [x] Under chronic high-intensity exercise, IL-6 tends to be pro-inflammatory, via a different signalling mechanism.
- [ ] IL-6 is an anti-inflammatory cytokine in all circumstances, at rest and in exercise.

> The IL-6 gene is silent in resting muscle and rapidly activated in exercise; the IL-6 of acute exercise is anti-inflammatory and decreases TNF-α, but under chronic high-intensity exercise IL-6 tends to be pro-inflammatory, through a different signalling mechanism, and contributes to low-grade inflammation. See [[IL-6 in Exercise#1. Two sources, two faces]], [[IL-6 in Exercise#5. The IL-6 paradox]] and [[Chronic Exercise and Immune Function – Moderate versus High Intensity#3. Chronic high-intensity exercise]].
