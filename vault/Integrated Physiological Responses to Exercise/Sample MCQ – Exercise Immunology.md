Sample multiple-choice questions on the immune system and its response to exercise. Each answer comes with a short explanation and the note that covers it.

## Which antibody subtype is mainly found in saliva?

- [ ] IgG
- [ ] IgD
- [ ] IgM
- [x] IgA

> IgA is the main antibody in saliva and other secretions, where it stops microbes before they enter the body, which is why exercise studies measure salivary IgA. IgG makes up most of the antibody in the blood, IgM is the first antibody made in a new infection, and IgD sits with IgM on the surface of B cells. See [[B Cells and Antibodies]].

## In relation to immune function, what are the complement proteins?

- [x] Circulating plasma proteins that are activated in the presence of a pathogen.
- [ ] Small proteins secreted from immune cells during infection.
- [ ] Specific cytokine receptors that are found on immune cells.
- [ ] Intracellular signalling molecules that mediate alterations to cell function.

> Complement is a set of about 30 plasma proteins, made mostly by the liver, that normally circulate in an inactive state and are activated in a cascade in the presence of a pathogen. Small proteins that cells secrete to carry messages are cytokines, which are easily confused with complement. See [[Complement and Natural Killer Cells]] and [[Cytokines]].

## When lymphocytes migrate from the blood to the tissues, they interact with __________ in order to cross the blood vessel wall.

- [ ] cytokines
- [ ] toll-like receptors
- [x] cell adhesion molecules
- [ ] transcription factors

> Cell adhesion molecules work in pairs, one on the lymphocyte binding a partner on the endothelial cells that line the vessel, and they take the cell through rolling, firm adhesion and diapedesis into the tissue. Chemokines, a kind of cytokine, attract lymphocytes and help them grip, but the crossing itself depends on the adhesion molecules, while toll-like receptors are for recognising microbes. See [[Mechanisms of Exercise-Induced Immune Changes]] and [[Cytokines]].

## During prolonged, intense endurance exercise, which cytokine shows the most significant increase in plasma concentration?

- [ ] IL-2
- [ ] IL-10
- [x] IL-6
- [ ] IFN-γ

> Contracting muscle releases so much IL-6 that its plasma level rises up to 100-fold after a marathon, more than any other cytokine rises from its resting level. It also comes first, peaking around the end of exercise, and the anti-inflammatory IL-1ra peaks after it. See [[IL-6 in Exercise]].

## Regarding the function of neutrophils, which of the following is INCORRECT?

- [ ] They are present and functional from birth.
- [x] They develop and adapt throughout life.
- [ ] They respond to infection immediately.
- [ ] They recognise foreign pathogens in a non-specific manner.

> Neutrophils belong to the innate branch, which is in place from birth, acts at once and recognises patterns shared by whole groups of microbes, so the other three statements describe them. Developing and adapting over a lifetime, as each antigen met selects and multiplies the lymphocytes that recognise it and leaves memory cells behind, is the hallmark of adaptive immunity, that is of B and T cells. See [[Cells and tissues of the immune system]] and [[Immune System Overview – Functions and the Two Branches]].

## Which of the following cell types undergoes clonal selection following interaction with antigen?

- [ ] Monocyte
- [ ] Neutrophil
- [x] T cell
- [ ] Basophil

> Clonal selection needs receptors for a single antigen, which only T and B cells carry. The antigen selects the few lymphocytes whose receptor fits it, which for a T cell means a fragment displayed on MHC by an antigen-presenting cell, and these divide into a clone of effector and memory cells. Monocytes, neutrophils and basophils are innate cells and don't undergo it. See [[Adaptive Immunity – Principles and Clonal Selection]] and [[T Cells and Antigen Presentation]].

## According to the J shaped curve, very high intensity exercise causes an above-average risk of

- [ ] urinary tract infections.
- [ ] secretion of inflammatory markers.
- [x] upper respiratory tract infections.
- [ ] generalised immune dysregulation.

> Nieman's J-shaped curve relates the amount and intensity of exercise to the risk of upper respiratory tract infection (URTI), such as a cold. Taking a sedentary person's risk as average, moderate workloads are associated with better immunosurveillance and a below-average risk, and unusually high workloads with poorer immunosurveillance and an above-average risk. The curve is debated, since not all studies support it and only about a third of athletes' upper respiratory symptoms turn out to be infections. See [[Exercise and Infection Risk – J-Shaped Curve and Open Window]].

## Which of the following is NOT associated with chronic exercise done at moderate intensity with adequate recovery?

- [x] Higher baseline pro-inflammatory cytokines.
- [ ] Increased T cells, including T regulatory cells.
- [ ] Slower age-related immune decline.
- [ ] More efficient immune cell trafficking.

> The opposite is true: moderate training with adequate recovery lowers baseline pro-inflammatory cytokines and raises anti-inflammatory ones, which reduces chronic low-grade inflammation and lowers resting CRP. The other three are all adaptations to such training. See [[Chronic Exercise and Immune Function – Moderate versus High Intensity]].
