### 1. T cells need an introduction

A T cell (T lymphocyte) recognises antigen with its ==T cell receptors (TCRs)==, surface proteins that are ==highly specific==, each fitting one antigen only. Yet a TCR ==cannot recognise a free microbe==, only a fragment displayed by another cell, so for pathogen recognition ==T cells need to be ‘introduced’ to antigen by an antigen-presenting cell (APC)==.

The APCs are ==dendritic cells, macrophages and neutrophils==. Each engulfs the pathogen by ==phagocytosis== ([[Innate Immunity – Pathogen Recognition and Phagocytosis]]), digests it, and uses the ==Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) to display fragments of the digested antigen on its surface==. MHC is a family of surface proteins that work like display stands, each ==holding up a small piece of antigen (a peptide) to T cells==, and the TCR recognises this ==antigen presented by MHC==.

![[Screenshot 2026-10-23 at 13.38.03.png]]

The cartoon reads left to right. In step 1 a phagocyte (here a dendritic cell) ‘eats’ a bacterium, drawn green with its antigen in red. In step 2 parts of the bacterium (the antigen) go to the phagocyte’s surface, where in reality MHC holds them. In step 3 the phagocyte presents the antigen to a helper T cell (purple, see section 2), which in step 4 is activated. The T cell never meets the whole bacterium, only a fragment, and cytotoxic T cells, though not drawn, are switched on the same way.

Because APCs are innate cells and T cells adaptive ones, this hand-over is ==how the innate branch instructs the adaptive branch==, the feature listed in [[Immune System Overview – Functions and the Two Branches]]. It also explains why [[Cells and Tissues of the Immune System]] described the dendritic cell as the cell that detects pathogens and presents them to lymphocytes. The activated T cell then multiplies into a clone by ==clonal selection== ([[Adaptive Immunity – Principles and Clonal Selection]]).

### 2. Three kinds of T cell

What the clone does next depends on which of the ==three main types of T cell== it belongs to. ==Helper T cells produce cytokines==, small signalling proteins ([[Cytokines]]), ==to increase the activity of B cells, macrophages and other T cells==, so they are the organisers. Because macrophages are innate cells, this help reaches back into the innate branch, while the help given to B cells is picked up in [[B Cells and Antibodies]].

Where helper T cells organise, ==cytotoxic (killer) T cells kill foreign cells or infected host cells== by releasing ==perforin and granzyme==, the same weapons as natural killer (NK) cells ([[Complement and Natural Killer Cells]]). The difference is that a cytotoxic T cell is ==specific for one antigen and must first be presented with it==, whereas an NK cell needs neither.

![[Screenshot 2026-10-23 at 13.38.32.png|385]]

This scanning electron micrograph shows a ==killer T cell (left) attacking a cancer cell (right)==. The small T cell has latched onto the larger, lumpy cancer cell, showing that killer T cells kill by direct contact and also target body cells that turn cancerous.

The third type works in reverse, because ==regulatory T cells (Tregs) suppress the immune response==. They produce the ==anti-inflammatory cytokine interleukin-10 (IL-10)==, which calms immune cells rather than stirring them up, so Tregs are the brakes. Tregs and IL-10 both return in the exercise notes.

| T cell type | What it does | What it releases |
|---|---|---|
| Helper T cell | Organiser: boosts B cells, macrophages and other T cells | Cytokines |
| Cytotoxic (killer) T cell | Kills foreign or infected host cells | Perforin and granzyme |
| Regulatory T cell (Treg) | Brake: suppresses the immune response | IL-10 (anti-inflammatory) |

### Summary

> T cell receptors are highly specific but ==cannot recognise a free microbe==, so APCs (==dendritic cells, macrophages and neutrophils==) phagocytose it and present its fragments on ==MHC==. This is ==how the innate branch instructs the adaptive branch==, and the activated T cell then multiplies by ==clonal selection==. ==Helper T cells release cytokines== that boost B cells, macrophages and other T cells. ==Cytotoxic (killer) T cells kill foreign, infected or cancerous cells with perforin and granzyme==, and ==regulatory T cells (Tregs) suppress the response with the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10==.
