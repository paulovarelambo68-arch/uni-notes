
> The anterolateral system is the ascending system of the cord that carries ==pain and temperature== to the brain, the pain and temperature arm of the [[Ascending Tracts]]. From the thalamus onward it works as ==two parallel pathways== that share the same start: the Aδ and C afferents synapse in the dorsal horn, and the second order axons cross and ascend to the thalamus. They differ in where they relay and where they end. The ==spinothalamic tract==, the component that runs directly from cord to thalamus, ==is the sensory discriminative route==: the tract is the anatomy and the discriminative aspect is its function,. The motivational and affective dimension travels in the ==remaining projections of the system, which have no single tract name==, relays in the ==medial thalamic nuclei== and ends in the cingulate cortex, the insula and the prefrontal cortex.
 
### 1. The anterolateral system and the spinothalamic tract

The anterolateral system is the ==ascending system for pain and temperature==, named after its position in the anterolateral white matter of the spinal cord, and the ==spinothalamic tract== is its direct component, the one that links the cord with the thalamus. Pain and temperature afferents (the [[Classification of Nerve Fibres|Aδ and C fibres]]) enter the cord from the ==lower body at lumbar levels== and from the ==upper body, excluding the face, at cervical levels==, and synapse on second order neurons in the dorsal horn. The axons of these neurons ==cross to the opposite side== and ascend as the spinothalamic tract through the brain stem (caudal and middle medulla, pons and midbrain) to synapse in the ==ventral posterior lateral nucleus of the thalamus==, from which third order neurons reach the ==primary somatic sensory cortex==. This is why ==the spinothalamic tract and the sensory discriminative aspect are the same pathway==: the tract names the anatomy, the aspect names the function.

What that function analyses is ==where the pain is, how intense it is and what it feels like: location, intensity and quality==. ==Quality is the character of the sensation==, whether the pain feels sharp, dull, burning or aching. Within the [[Somatosensory Cortex]] the signal is distributed between ==[[SI]] and [[SII]]==: ==SI receives the fine spatial and intensive detail of the stimulus==, resolving exactly where it is and how strong, while ==SII elaborates the information further==, contributing to the recognition and interpretation of the stimulus. A stimulus that is ==localised== is one the person can assign to a precise point of the body, and a stimulus that is ==characterised== is one whose intensity and quality are consciously identified.

### 2. The motivational and affective dimension of pain

The motivational and affective dimension covers the ==cognitive, emotional and motor aspects== of the experience: how the pain is judged, the unpleasantness it carries and the behaviour it provokes. ==This route has no tract name of its own==: it is formed by the remaining projections of the anterolateral system, and their nociceptive drive reaches the ==medial nuclei of the thalamus==, from which the third order neurons project to each target. They terminate in the ==prefrontal cortex== and the anterior cingulate cortex, where the signal is evaluated and acquires its emotional weight, and in the ==insula==, which senses the internal state of the body and gives pain its felt unpleasantness, as opposed to its location. They also reach the ==amygdala and hippocampus==, which attach feeling and memory to the experience, and they engage the ==descending pain pathway==, which projects back down to the dorsal horn and modulates the nociceptive input arriving from the Aδ and C fibres. The motor aspect is served by the motor cortex and the cerebellum: the cerebellum is activated by pain not to perceive it but to coordinate the movements that pain provokes, such as withdrawal, guarding and escape.

### 3. Worked example: burning the top of a finger

Touching a hot surface with the top of a finger runs the whole pathway in order. The heat opens the ==TRPV1 channels at their threshold of about 43 °C== in the nociceptive free nerve endings (see [[Thermoreceptors and Nociceptors]]), transducing the stimulus into action potentials in the [[Classification of Nerve Fibres|Aδ and C fibres]], whose ==cell bodies sit in the dorsal root ganglion== (see [[Pain and Types of Pain]]). Their central axons enter the cord at ==cervical level==, since the finger belongs to the upper body, and terminate in their fibre specific laminae of the dorsal horn, ==Aδ in laminae I and V and C in laminae I and II==, where the terminals release ==glutamate and substance P== onto the second order neurons (see [[Pain in the Spinal Cord and the Withdrawal Reflex]]). The axons of these neurons ==cross to the opposite side within the cord== and ascend as the ==spinothalamic tract== through the brain stem to the ==ventral posterior lateral nucleus== of the thalamus, from which the third order neurons reach ==SI and SII== of the [[Somatosensory Cortex]], where the burn is localised to the fingertip and characterised as burning. In parallel, and without waiting for any of this, the Aδ input drives the ==flexion (withdrawal) reflex== at spinal level, contracting the agonist and silencing the antagonist, so the finger is pulled away before the pain is even consciously felt.

### Summary

>The anterolateral system is the ascending system for pain and temperature. Its direct component, the spinothalamic tract, is the sensory discriminative route, the same pathway named by anatomy and by function: afferents synapse on second order neurons in the dorsal horn, whose axons cross and ascend through the brain stem to the ventral posterior lateral nucleus of the thalamus, with third order neurons continuing to SI, which resolves precisely where the stimulus is and how strong, and to SII, which elaborates its recognition. The motivational and affective dimension has no tract name: the remaining projections relay through the medial thalamic nuclei and distribute the signal to the prefrontal cortex, the anterior cingulate cortex, the insula, the amygdala and the hippocampus for the cognitive, emotional and motor aspects of pain, and engage the descending pain pathway, which projects back to the cord to modulate incoming nociceptive signals.
