
>The anterolateral system is the ascending pathway that carries ==pain and temperature== from the spinal cord to the brain, and its principal component is the ==spinothalamic tract==. After relaying in the thalamus, pain is handled along ==two parallel pathways==: a sensory discriminative aspect and a motivational and affective dimension. This is the pain and temperature arm of the [[Ascending Tracts]].

### 1. The anterolateral system and the spinothalamic tract

Pain and temperature afferents (the [[Classification of Nerve Fibres|Aδ and C fibres]]) enter the spinal cord, from the ==lower body at lumbar levels== and from the ==upper body, excluding the face, at cervical levels==, and synapse on second order neurons in the dorsal horn. The axons of these second order neurons ==cross to the opposite side of the cord== and ascend in the anterolateral white matter as the ==spinothalamic tract==, climbing through the brain stem (caudal and middle medulla, pons and midbrain) to synapse in the ==ventral posterior lateral nucleus of the thalamus==. From there, third order neurons project to the ==primary somatic sensory cortex==.

### 2. The sensory discriminative aspect of pain

This pathway analyses ==where it hurts, how much and in what way: location, intensity and quality==. The ==second order neurons synapse in the thalamus==, and the ==third order neurons project to SI and SII==, the primary and secondary areas of the [[Somatosensory Cortex]], where the stimulus is localised and characterised.

### 3. The motivational and affective dimension of pain

This pathway carries the ==cognitive, emotional and motor aspects== of pain: how the stimulus is interpreted, the unpleasant feeling it evokes and the behaviour it drives. Here the ==third order neurons terminate in the prefrontal cortex, the amygdala, the hippocampus and the descending pain pathway==, connecting the nociceptive signal to judgement, emotion, memory and the brain's own pain control system.

### Summary

>The anterolateral system carries pain and temperature from the cord to the brain through the spinothalamic tract: afferents synapse on second order neurons in the cord, whose axons cross and ascend through the brain stem to the ventral posterior lateral nucleus of the thalamus, from which third order neurons reach the primary somatic sensory cortex. Pain is then processed in parallel: the sensory discriminative aspect (location, intensity and quality) relays through the thalamus to SI and SII, while the motivational and affective dimension (cognitive, emotional and motor aspects) projects from the thalamus to the prefrontal cortex, amygdala, hippocampus and the descending pain pathway.
