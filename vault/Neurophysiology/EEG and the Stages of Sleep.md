
>The **electroencephalogram**, or **EEG**, is a record of the ==electrical potential changes of the brain==, produced by the ==synchronous firing== of large populations of cortical neurons. It appears as ==waves of different frequency and amplitude==, and it is the recording by which the ==stages of sleep== are defined.

### The EEG and Synchrony

Each scalp electrode sums the activity of thousands of neurons, so the trace depends on their timing. Independently firing cells fluctuate out of phase and cancel, leaving only a small, fast residue; populations firing together arrive in phase and summate into large waves, but can only change as fast as the single rhythm driving them, which in sleep is slow. Both properties therefore follow from one cause: ==the more synchronised the neuronal firing, the larger the amplitude and the lower the frequency==. Hence the alert brain, desynchronised, gives a fast, low voltage trace, while deep sleep, synchronised to one slow beat, gives a slow, high voltage one.

### EEG Rhythms

Four named rhythms mark the descent from waking into sleep. In the **alert** brain the **beta rhythm** (β) dominates, fast and small at ==13 to 60 Hz== and ==5 to 10 µV==. In **inattention**, relaxed waking with the eyes closed, the **alpha rhythm** (α) takes over at ==8 to 13 Hz== and ==30 to 50 µV==. **Sleep and anaesthesia** bring the slow rhythms of large amplitude: **theta** (θ) at ==4 to 7 Hz==, and **delta** (δ) at ==0.5 to 4 Hz== with an amplitude ==over 75 µV==.

### The Division of Sleep

Sleep is ==not merely a state of reduced brain activity== but an actively regulated succession of states, each with its own EEG pattern. A night's sleep divides into ==slow wave sleep==, which accounts for ==75%==, and ==rapid eye movement sleep== (REM), which accounts for ==25%==. By current consensus the non REM stages number ==three, N1 to N3==, where four were distinguished before. How these stages cycle across the night is treated in [[The Sleep Cycle Across the Night and Across Life]], and REM itself in [[REM versus Slow Wave Sleep]].

### The Stages of Slow Wave Sleep

**N1** is the lightest stage, the transition into sleep: the rhythm slows to ==4 to 6 Hz==, entering the ==theta band==, and the ==amplitude increases==.

**N2** deepens the trend, with more irregular and slower waves, ==1 to 5 Hz== straddling ==theta and delta==, of still larger amplitude, and against this slow background ==three signature events stand out==. The first are **sleep spindles**, short bursts of rhythmic activity at ==12 to 16 Hz==, ==alpha like== in frequency but of ==thalamic origin==, whose function is ==not fully understood==. Alongside them appear **K-complexes**, large, isolated waveforms that rise above the background rhythm and are ==particularly evident in frontal cortex==; a K-complex can be ==evoked by a meaningful stimulus==, such as the sleeper's own name or a baby crying, as if the sleeping brain were deciding whether to wake. The third event belongs to a different scale altogether: **hippocampal ripples**, very brief oscillations of ==80 to 120 Hz== generated in the hippocampus, faster even than beta.

**N3** is the deepest stage, proper slow wave sleep, where the rhythm settles fully into ==delta==: the ==slowest and largest waves==, ==0.5 to 3 Hz== at ==over 75 µV==, while spindles become ==very rare== and there are ==no K-complexes==.

### Summary

>The EEG turns sleep into a readable sequence: as cortical firing synchronises, the waves slow and grow, shifting from beta and alpha in waking to theta and delta in sleep. The core mapping to retain is slow wave sleep descending through ==N1, N2 and N3== over ==75%== of the night, with the remaining ==25%== given to REM.
