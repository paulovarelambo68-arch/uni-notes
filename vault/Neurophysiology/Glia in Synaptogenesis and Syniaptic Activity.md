
> Glia are active partners at the synapse, not passive support cells. In the CNS, synapses only form and persist when astrocytes or other macroglia are present, and glia also sense and respond to ongoing synaptic activity.

### 1. Role of glia in synaptogenesis

Neurons migrate into position during development, but ==synapse formation only occurs when astrocytes (or other macroglia) are present==. The astrocytes release ==extracellular protein signals== that trigger synapse formation in the CNS. The dependence is not limited to development: adult hippocampal stem cells need astrocytes to form synapses as well. And once built, synapses still rely on glia, because macroglia are ==necessary for synapse maintenance==, not only for formation.

### 2. Macroglia actively control synaptogenesis

This control is active rather than permissive: astrocytes and other macroglia regulate ==synapse number, synapse stability and synapse function==. The principle extends beyond the CNS, since there is evidence that **Schwann cells** in the periphery trigger ==neuromuscular junction formation==.

### 3. Role in synaptic activity

Glia (in practice astrocytes, as shown in culture) also take part in ongoing signalling, and they can do so because glial cells carry neurotransmitter receptors (mostly metabotropic ones, coupled to [[intracellular cascades]]). When transmitter is released during [[Synaptic Transmission|synaptic transmission]], the glial cell responds as well: the ==calcium transient currents== are brief rises of intracellular Ca²⁺ inside the glial cell, and they are the measurable sign that the glia has ==sensed synaptic activity==. This Ca²⁺ then works as a second messenger within the glial cell, and the signal can even propagate as a calcium wave, both within one glial cell and between neighbouring glia.

The purpose of that calcium signal is to trigger the glial cell to ==release gliotransmitters in response to neuronal activity==. What they signal back onto is the synapse itself: gliotransmitters can regulate how much transmitter the presynaptic terminal releases, or alter the responsiveness of the postsynaptic cell, and through this they can also influence activity dependent plastic changes. In other words, synaptic signalling is not a private dialogue between two neurons: the glial cell listens, and talks back to tune the transmission.
### Summary

> Astrocytes and other macroglia are required for both the formation (through extracellular protein signals) and the maintenance of CNS synapses, and they actively regulate synapse number, stability and function, with Schwann cells playing the equivalent role at the neuromuscular junction. Glia also sense synaptic activity through calcium transients and answer it by releasing gliotransmitters, making them active participants at the synapse.
