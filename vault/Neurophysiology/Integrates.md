Neurons receive thousands of synaptic inputs at once. Integration = the soma/dendrites **summate** all these incoming graded potentials, both in space and in time:

- **Spatial summation:** inputs arriving simultaneously at different dendritic locations add together.
- **Temporal summation:** repeated inputs from the same synapse arriving in rapid succession stack up before decaying.

**Example:** Your biceps motor neuron in the spinal cord receives excitatory input from the cortex ("contract") and inhibitory input from an interneuron in the stretch reflex of the antagonist triceps ("relax"). Each input produces a small voltage change (~0.1–1 mV). Individually, none is enough to fire the neuron, but if enough excitatory inputs arrive close together and outweigh the inhibitory ones, the summed depolarisation reaches threshold and the motor neuron fires, producing a muscle contraction. The soma is essentially a summing junction.
