
> ==Language is a lateralised cortical function, dominated by the left hemisphere==, while the other hemisphere is concerned with ==spatial and temporal relations==. A small connected network of areas in the dominant hemisphere implements the comprehension and production of language.

### Hemisphere dominance

==The left hemisphere is dominant for language== in the great majority of people, and the ==other hemisphere is concerned with spatial and temporal relations==. This dominance is not absolute: ==a small percentage of people, typically left handed, have right hemisphere language dominance instead==.

### Evidence for dominance

Four lines of evidence establish the dominance. The first comes from ==aphasias==, language disorders that arise from damage to the language areas of the left hemisphere; these disorders are covered in [[Aphasias and Speaking Pathways]]. The second is ==imaging and direct recording==, in which ==**[[PET]]**, **[[fMRI]]** and the **direct recording of electrical activity during surgery**== show language tasks activating left hemisphere areas. The third is the ==**Wada procedure**==, in which ==one hemisphere is anaesthetised==, revealing whether the awake hemisphere alone can sustain language. The fourth comes from ==**split brain** patients==, in whom the ==corpus callosum has been severed==, so the two hemispheres can no longer communicate and each can be studied independently.

### The language implementation system

Four areas form a connected map, each anchored to a nearby sensory or motor region. ==**Wernicke's area**== lies in the ==posterior part of the superior temporal lobe, just behind the auditory cortex==, and handles the ==comprehension of auditory and visual information==. The ==**arcuate fasciculus**==, the fibre tract that ==connects Wernicke's area with Broca's area==, carries this understood information forwards into the frontal lobe. ==**Broca's area**== lies ==in the frontal lobe, in front of the lower part of the motor cortex==, and ==plans and coordinates vocalisation==. The ==**angular gyrus**== lies in the parietal lobe, above and behind Wernicke's area and just in front of the visual cortex, functionally, it ==converts written words into their verbal form== by matching visual symbols from reading to their auditory language representation, which is why damage to it produces some ==dyslexias==.

### Summary

> Language is dominated by the left hemisphere, with the other hemisphere handling spatial and temporal relations and a small, typically left handed minority showing right dominance, as shown by aphasias, imaging and direct recording, the Wada procedure and split brain patients. The implementation system runs from Wernicke's area behind the auditory cortex, which comprehends auditory and visual information, through the arcuate fasciculus to Broca's area in front of the motor cortex, which plans and coordinates vocalisation, with the angular gyrus near the visual cortex implicated in some dyslexias.
