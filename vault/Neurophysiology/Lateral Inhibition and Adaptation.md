
> Lateral inhibition and adaptation are two coding strategies of the somatosensory system. ==Lateral inhibition enhances the quality of sensory information== by suppressing the pathways that flank the most active one, while ==adaptation codes for stimulus duration== through the way receptor firing declines, or persists, during a sustained stimulus.

### 1. Lateral inhibition

A localised stimulus, like a pin on the skin, activates several primary sensory neurons at once, and the response of each one is proportional to stimulus strength: the pathway closest to the stimulus fires the most, its immediate neighbours less, and distant ones remain at their tonic level. At the relay, ==the pathway closest to the stimulus inhibits its neighbours==, pushing the flanking pathways below that tonic level. The effect is contrast enhancement: ==inhibition of the lateral neurons enhances perception of the stimulus==, because the difference between the strongly active central pathway and its silenced surroundings is exaggerated. This is how lateral inhibition sharpens the quality of sensory information.

### 2. Adaptation

Adaptation describes how the receptor potential and firing rate evolve while a stimulus is maintained, and it is ==the code for stimulus duration==. Receptors split into two classes by their temporal response, rapidly adapting and slowly adapting.

### 3. Rapidly adapting receptors

In rapidly adapting receptors ==the receptor potential decreases in response to a sustained stimulus==, so the afferent produces only an on-off burst of [[The Action Potential|action potentials]] at the start (and end) of stimulation and then falls silent. Because of this they ==convey information on changes in ongoing stimulation== rather than on the steady stimulus itself. The classic example is the [[Pacinian corpuscle]].

### 4. Slowly adapting receptors

Slowly adapting receptors behave the opposite way: they keep firing with ==sustained activity for as long as the stimulus is present==. This makes them suited to ==convey information on the spatial attributes of the stimulus, such as size and shape==. The example given is [[nociceptors]].

### Summary

> Lateral inhibition enhances the quality of sensory information: the pathway closest to the stimulus inhibits its neighbours, exaggerating contrast and sharpening perception of the stimulus. Adaptation codes for stimulus duration: rapidly adapting receptors, like the Pacinian corpuscle, lose their receptor potential during a sustained stimulus and fire only an on-off burst of action potentials, signalling changes in ongoing stimulation, while slowly adapting receptors, like nociceptors, fire throughout the stimulus and signal its spatial attributes, such as size and shape.
