
>==Sleep disorders are disturbances of the normal sleep cycle== experienced by a substantial fraction of the population at some point in life, and they range from the merely annoying to life threatening. The most significant are ==insomnia==, the ==parasomnias== and ==narcolepsy==, a chronic disorder in which the loss of ==orexin (hypocretin) signalling== lets sleep intrude into the waking day.

### Prevalence

==Insomnia is the inability to sleep for long enough, or deeply enough, to produce a subjective sense of refreshment==, and it is by far the most common sleep disorder, affecting about **15%** of the population. The ==parasomnias==, abnormal behaviours that intrude into sleep, together account for roughly **10%**. Within this group, ==excessive sleepiness== affects about **2%**, ==somnambulism== (sleepwalking) **2.5%**, ==sleep talking== **6%** and ==bedwetting== **3%**, while ==narcolepsy== is rare at only **0.05%**. The remaining parasomnias are ==confusional arousals==, ==night terror attacks==, ==incubus== and ==REM behaviour disorder==, together with ==sleep paralysis==, in which the ==muscle atonia of REM sleep persists into wakefulness==, leaving the sleeper conscious yet unable to move.

### Orexin and hypocretin

==Orexin, also called hypocretin, is a peptide released by hypothalamic neurones== whose axons terminate in areas such as the **brainstem** and the **cerebrum**. In the brainstem it **regulates cholinergic and aminergic neurones**, and through these targets it plays a role in ==sleep and wakefulness, vigilance and hunger==: it ==promotes wakefulness== and, in parallel, **increases food intake**. Consistent with this, ==levels of hypocretin or orexin are reduced in the cerebrospinal fluid of human narcoleptics==, directly linking the symptoms to the loss of this peptide.

### Causes of narcolepsy

The cause differs between species. In ==dogs, narcolepsy is genetic==, produced by a ==mutation of the orexin receptor==, so the peptide is present but cannot act. In ==humans, narcolepsy is an autoimmune disease== in which the ==orexin releasing neurones are destroyed==, which explains why the peptide itself is reduced.

### Symptoms of narcolepsy

The disorder shows itself as ==irresistible sleep episodes== and ==excessive daytime sleepiness==, together with ==cataplexy==, a sudden loss of muscle control while awake. Sleep also takes an abnormal shape: ==REM sleep begins rapidly==, entered directly from wakefulness, and dreams are ==very lucid==. These features can be framed as a possible ==imbalance of [[Neurochemistry and Brainstem Control of Sleep | cholinergic and aminergic activity]] in the brainstem==: without orexin regulation, the switch that normally separates wakefulness from REM sleep becomes unstable, so that sleep, with its dreams and its loss of muscle tone, invades waking life. 

### Summary

>Sleep disorders are widespread: insomnia, the inability to sleep enough to feel refreshed, leads at 15%, the parasomnias follow at about 10%, and narcolepsy is rare at 0.05%. Narcolepsy arises from failed orexin signalling, through an autoimmune destruction of the orexin neurones in humans. Losing this hypothalamic input unbalances brainstem cholinergic and aminergic activity, producing irresistible sleep episodes, daytime sleepiness, cataplexy, rapid REM onset and very lucid dreams.
