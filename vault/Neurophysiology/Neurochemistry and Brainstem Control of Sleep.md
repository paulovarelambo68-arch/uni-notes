
> ==Sleep is governed by the chemistry and circuitry of the brainstem==: sleep promoting and wake promoting biochemicals gate the onset of **SWS**, the **reticular formation** controls arousal, and a switch between brainstem nuclei governs the alternation between **SWS** and **REM**. 

### Onset of SWS

The trigger for the onset of SWS is unresolved: a ==sleep inducing peptide== is suspected, meaning a substance produced within the body itself that would actively promote sleep, and many biochemicals are implicated, many of which have an immune role. Among them, **GABA**, the main inhibitory neurotransmitter of the brain, and **adenosine**, a byproduct of cellular energy use, ==increase sleep==, while **histamine**, a [[monoamine]] also active in immune responses, ==decreases sleep==.

### The reticular formation

The **reticular formation** is a ==network of neurones in the brainstem== that can ==influence arousal through the Reticular Activating System==. The two ends of the brainstem have opposing roles: the ==rostral brainstem (upper) is necessary for wakefulness==, the ==caudal brainstem (lower) for sleep==. Its fibres run in both directions: ==ascending fibres innervate the cerebrum==, including the **hypothalamus, thalamus and forebrain**, while ==descending fibres innervate the body==, such as **skeletal muscle**.

The figure shows this network in the core of the brainstem in a sagittal view of the brain. Sensory input converges on it from below, the ascending general sensory tracts carrying touch, pain and temperature, joined by visual and auditory impulses, and its output leaves in both directions: radiations fan upwards to widespread areas of the cerebral cortex, while descending motor projections run down to the spinal cord. The reticular formation thus lies between the sensory inflow and its two targets, the cerebrum above and the body below.

==Stimulating the rostral brainstem can wake a person even under anaesthesia==: its ascending drive to the **thalamus and forebrain** restores arousal.

### The switch between SWS and REM

The transition between SWS and REM behaves as an on off switch between **brainstem nuclei**, a nucleus being a cluster of neurones with a shared function. One side of the switch is **cholinergic**, meaning its neurones release the neurotransmitter **acetylcholine**: ==acetylcholine induces REM==, so the cholinergic neurones are the ==REM on cells==, ==active in REM==. They sit in two nuclei of the tegmentum, the core region of the brainstem: the **laterodorsal tegmental nucleus** and the **pedunculopontine tegmental nucleus**. The other side is **aminergic**, meaning its neurones release [[monoamine]] neurotransmitters: the **dorsal raphe nucleus**, a midline brainstem nucleus releasing **serotonin**, and the **locus ceruleus**, a small brainstem nucleus releasing **noradrenaline**. These aminergic neurones are the ==REM off cells==, ==quiescent in REM== and active in SWS, though ==less so than in wakefulness==.

### The neurochemistry of REM

REM sleep appears to be driven by ==phasic bursts of activity==, brief surges of neuronal firing rather than a steady rhythm. The clearest marker of these bursts is the **PGO spike**, a sharp electrical signal that begins in the **pons**, a region of the brainstem, in its dorsolateral part, the **nucleus reticularis pontis oralis** of the reticular formation. From there the spikes ==propagate rostrally==, towards the front of the brain, reaching first the **[[The Lateral Geniculate Nucleus|lateral geniculate nucleus]]** of the thalamus, the visual relay station, and then the **occipital cortex**, the visual region at the back of the brain: pons, geniculate, occipital, the three stations that give the spike its name. The spikes ==correlate with the onset of the rapid eye movements and the other changes of REM sleep==.

### Summary

> Sleep onset is chemical, with GABA and adenosine promoting sleep and histamine opposing it, while arousal is anatomical, the rostral brainstem sustaining wakefulness and the caudal brainstem enabling sleep through the ascending and descending fibres of the reticular formation. SWS and REM alternate through an on off switch of cholinergic REM on cells against aminergic REM off cells, and REM itself is driven by phasic PGO spikes running from the dorsolateral pons through the lateral geniculate nucleus to the occipital cortex.
