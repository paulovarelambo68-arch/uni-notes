

>It is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system; an **electrically excitable** cell specialised for receiving, integrating, and transmitting information via electrical (action potential) and chemical (synaptic) signals. Its main components are:


### Cell Body (Soma)

The metabolic and [[synthetic]] centre of the neuron. Contains the organelles (ribosomes, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum) responsible for protein synthesis and energy production. [[Integrates]] incoming signals and maintains the neuron's survival.

- **Subcomponent: Nucleus**, located within the soma; contains the genetic material (DNA) and controls all cellular activity, including gene expression for [[neurotransmitter synthesis]].
### Dendrites 

Branched, tree-like processes extending from the soma. They are the main _receptive_ structures, receiving incoming signals from other neurons at [[synapses]] and conducting them toward the cell body. Their extensive branching (as in cells B and C) vastly increases the surface area for synaptic input.
### Axon

A single, elongated projection that _conducts_ signals away from the soma to other neurons, muscles, or glands. Carries action potentials over distances ranging from millimetres to over a metre.

- **Subcomponent: Axon Hillock**, the cone-shaped junction between the soma and the axon. It acts as the neuron's "[[spike-initiation zone]]": it sums (integrates) all incoming [[excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic potentials]], and if the threshold is reached here, an action potential is generated.

>Dendrites receive → Soma integrates → Axon hillock triggers → Axon transmits.
