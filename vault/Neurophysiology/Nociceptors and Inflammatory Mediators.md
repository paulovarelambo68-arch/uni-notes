
>Nociceptors are the free nerve endings that detect noxious, potentially tissue damaging stimuli and give rise to pain. They fall into two classes distinguished by their axons and stimulus selectivity: ==Aδ nociceptors, about 20%, small diameter myelinated fibres responding to mechanical stimuli and tissue injury==, and ==C nociceptors, about 80%, small diameter unmyelinated fibres that are polymodal==, responding to mechanical, thermal and chemical stimuli. Their endings carry ==ion channel receptors tuned to each stimulus type==, and their sensitivity is raised by ==inflammatory mediators that reduce the pain threshold of the nerve==.

### 1. Aδ nociceptors

Aδ nociceptors make up about ==20% of nociceptors==. They respond to ==mechanical stimuli and tissue injury==, and their axons are ==small diameter myelinated fibres==, so they conduct relatively fast, exactly as diameter and myelination predict in [[Classification of Nerve Fibres]]. The pain they produce is ==sharp, prickling, bright and well localised==, the immediate "Ooch!!" that comes with ==withdrawal== from the stimulus.

### 2. C nociceptors

C nociceptors make up the remaining ==80%==. They are ==polymodal==, meaning a single ending responds to ==mechanical, thermal and chemical stimuli==. Their axons are ==small diameter unmyelinated fibres==, the slowest afferents in [[Sensory Receptors and Afferent Types]], and the pain they carry is ==slow, burning, aching and diffuse==, the lingering second pain that follows the sharp first impression.

### 3. Ion channel receptors on the free nerve endings

The free nerve endings of nociceptors express ==ion channel receptors== that transduce each class of noxious stimulus into a [[graded potential]]. The figure maps each stimulus to its channels: ==heat activates TRPV1, TRPV2, TRPV3 and TREK1==; ==chemical stimuli such as acid activate TRPV1, ASIC and DRASIC==; ==mechanical stimuli activate MDEG, DRASIC and TREK1==; and ==cold activates TRPM8==. TRPV1 appears in both the heat and chemical groups, fitting the polymodal character of the endings that express it.

### 4. Inflammatory mediators

Tissue injury releases a set of inflammatory mediators from damaged cells, mast cells and the nociceptor terminal itself, and ==these act to reduce the pain threshold of the nerve==. They split by their action on the nociceptor: ==histamine, substance P, potassium, serotonin and bradykinin activate the nociceptor directly==, while ==prostaglandins and leukotrienes sensitise it==, lowering its threshold so that weaker stimuli now produce pain. Prostaglandins and leukotrienes are synthesised from membrane phospholipids, which phospholipase converts to arachidonic acid, the substrate for two enzymes: cyclooxygenase yields prostaglandins and lipoxygenase yields leukotrienes. This is why ==COX inhibitors work as analgesics==: blocking cyclooxygenase cuts prostaglandin synthesis and removes the sensitisation.

### Summary

> Nociceptors are free nerve endings for noxious stimuli and come in two classes: Aδ nociceptors (20%, small myelinated fibres, mechanical stimuli and tissue injury, sharp prickling well localised pain with withdrawal) and C nociceptors (80%, small unmyelinated polymodal fibres responding to mechanical, thermal and chemical stimuli, slow burning aching diffuse pain). Their endings carry ion channel receptors matched to each stimulus: TRPV1, TRPV2, TRPV3 and TREK1 for heat, TRPV1, ASIC and DRASIC for chemical stimuli such as acid, MDEG, DRASIC and TREK1 for mechanical stimuli, and TRPM8 for cold. Inflammatory mediators released after injury reduce the pain threshold of the nerve: histamine, substance P, potassium, serotonin and bradykinin activate the nociceptor, while prostaglandins and leukotrienes, synthesised from arachidonic acid by cyclooxygenase and lipoxygenase, sensitise it, which is the target of COX inhibitors as analgesics.
