
> The axons of **retinal ganglion cells** form the optic nerve, and these cells fall into ==two major functional classes: P cells and M cells==, with an additional third type, the K ganglion cells. The classes differ in receptive field size, conduction speed and the kind of visual information they carry, which is the anatomical basis of the parallel pathways of vision.

### P cells

**P cells** belong to the **parvocellular division** and are the ==most numerous== of the ganglion cell classes. Each has a ==small receptive field==, which means it samples a tiny patch of the retina with fine precision. They are ==colour sensitive== and underlie ==high visual acuity and high spatial resolution==, so the P cell stream carries the detail and colour of the visual scene.

### M cells

**M cells** belong to the **magnocellular division** and have ==large receptive fields==, pooling input from a wide patch of retina. They are ==fast conducting== and are specialised for ==motion perception and rapidly changing detail==, giving ==high temporal resolution==. Where P cells resolve fine spatial detail, M cells resolve fine timing: the M cell stream tells the brain where things are moving and how fast the scene is changing. 

The class names come from the layers of the [[The Lateral Geniculate Nucleus|lateral geniculate nucleus]] each type feeds: ==M cells project to its large celled (magnocellular) layers, P cells to its small celled (parvocellular) layers==, so each division names the pathway the cell drives, not the cell itself.

### K ganglion cells

Beyond the two major classes, ==K ganglion cells are an additional type identified that transmit aspects of colour==. As noted in class, ==they carry blue light information==, complementing the colour sensitivity of the P cell stream.

### Summary

> Ganglion cell axons form the optic nerve and belong to two major classes. P cells of the parvocellular division are numerous, with small receptive fields, colour sensitivity, high visual acuity and high spatial resolution; M cells of the magnocellular division have large receptive fields, conduct fast, and handle motion perception and rapidly changing detail with high temporal resolution. K ganglion cells are an additional type transmitting aspects of colour, notably blue light information.
