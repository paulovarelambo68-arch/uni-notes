
> The IASP (International Association for the Study of Pain) defines pain as ==an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with potential or actual tissue damage, or described in terms of such damage==. Pain is therefore not just a sensation but also an emotional experience, and actual tissue damage is not required for it to be felt.

### Nociception versus pain

==Nociception is the process of transmitting a painful stimulus==: the neural activity that carries the signal from the site of injury up toward the central nervous system. It must not be confused with pain itself, because =="Pain is in the Brain"==: the unpleasant sensory and emotional experience only arises once the brain processes the incoming signal. Transmission of the stimulus and the experience of pain are two distinct steps.

### Acute somatic pain

Acute pain is divided into somatic and visceral. Acute somatic pain arises either from ==superficial== structures, such as the skin, or from ==deep== structures, such as connective tissue and bone. It is ==well localised==, plays a ==protective role== and ==promotes healing==.

### Acute visceral pain

Acute visceral pain arises from the ==internal organs==, triggered by ==distension of an organ==, as in colic, or by ==inflammation==, as in appendicitis. Unlike somatic pain, it is ==poorly localised==.

### Chronic pain

Chronic pain is ==long term, debilitating pain== accompanied by ==altered pain perception==. The examples given are ==rheumatoid arthritis== and ==phantom limb pain==.

### Summary

> Pain is an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with potential or actual tissue damage, or described in terms of such damage. Nociception, the process of transmitting a painful stimulus, is distinct from pain itself, which is experienced in the brain. Acute somatic pain, superficial or deep, is well localised, protective and promotes healing; acute visceral pain arises from distension or inflammation of internal organs and is poorly localised; chronic pain is long term and debilitating, with altered pain perception, as in rheumatoid arthritis and phantom limb pain.
