
> Pain afferents terminate in specific Rexed laminae of the dorsal horn: ==Aδ fibres in laminae I and V, C fibres in laminae I and II==. Lamina II, the ==substantia gelatinosa==, is a major site of ==pain modulation==, and the same nociceptive input also drives the ==flexion (withdrawal) reflex==, which removes the limb from a painful stimulus.

### 1. Where pain afferents terminate

The grey matter of the dorsal horn is organised into Rexed's laminae, and nociceptive afferents terminate in a lamina specific pattern. ==[[Classification of Nerve Fibres|Aδ fibres]] terminate in laminae I and V==, while ==[[Classification of Nerve Fibres|C fibres]] terminate in laminae I and II==. There they synapse onto the second order neurons of the pain pathway and onto local interneurons.

### 2. Lamina II: the substantia gelatinosa

==Lamina II corresponds to the substantia gelatinosa==, a layer rich in interneurons that is ==important in the modulation of pain==, since it can shape the nociceptive signal before it is relayed onward. The pain afferent terminals release excitatory neurotransmitters, principally ==glutamate and substance P==. These generate [[Postsynaptic Potentials|excitatory postsynaptic potentials]] (EPSPs) in the postsynaptic membrane, and when the EPSPs summate to threshold they trigger [[The Action Potential|action potentials]] in the second order neuron, so the pain signal is transmitted onward.

### 3. The flexion (withdrawal) reflex

The flexion, or withdrawal, reflex produces ==withdrawal from a painful stimulus==. ==Aδ activation leads to activation of excitatory and inhibitory interneurons in the spinal cord==: the excitatory interneurons drive the motor neurons supplying the agonist muscle, while the inhibitory interneurons silence the antagonist. The result is ==contraction of the agonist muscle and withdrawal from the stimulus==, a fast protective movement organised entirely at spinal level.

### Summary

> Pain afferents terminate lamina specifically in the dorsal horn: Aδ fibres in laminae I and V, C fibres in laminae I and II. Lamina II is the substantia gelatinosa, a key site of pain modulation, where afferent terminals release the excitatory transmitters glutamate and substance P, generating EPSPs that summate into action potentials in the second order neurons. The same Aδ input drives the flexion (withdrawal) reflex: excitatory and inhibitory interneurons in the cord produce contraction of the agonist muscle while the antagonist is inhibited, and the limb is withdrawn from the painful stimulus.
