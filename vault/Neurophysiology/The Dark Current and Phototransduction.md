
>The ==dark current== is the continuous influx of Na⁺ into the photoreceptor outer segment that keeps the cell depolarised at about ==−40 mV== in darkness, possible because cGMP holds the sodium channels open. **Phototransduction** is the cascade by which light lowers cGMP, closes these channels and hyperpolarises the cell, converting light into an electrical signal.

### The dark current

In darkness the photoreceptor rests in a depolarised state. **Na⁺ flows into the outer segment** through **[[cGMP]] gated sodium channels** (with some **Ca²⁺**, which the same channels admit), and this sodium influx is the ==dark current== itself: it depolarises the cell and sets a membrane potential of about ==−40 mV==. The channels stay open because ==cGMP splints them open==; as long as cGMP is bound, the inward current persists. Opposing it, **K⁺ flows out of the inner segment** through its own channels, so the steady −40 mV reflects the balance between sodium influx and potassium efflux.

As stressed in class, this resting potential is ==unusually high==: a typical neuron rests near −70 mV (see [[Membrane Potential]]), while the photoreceptor already sits partly depolarised at −40 mV before any stimulus arrives.

### The light cascade

Light activates the photopigment, and [[Photopigments and Colour Vision | activated rhodopsin]], **metarhodopsin II**, starts the cascade. Metarhodopsin II switches on the **G protein** ==transducin==, and transducin activates ==cGMP phosphodiesterase==, the enzyme to know by its full name, as stressed in class.

Two enzymes control cGMP levels: **guanylyl cyclase** which builds cGMP from **[[GTP]]**, while **cGMP phosphodiesterase** breaks cGMP down into **[[5′GMP]]**. The ==phosphodiesterase is active in the light==, so illumination tips the balance toward breakdown and **cGMP levels fall**. With cGMP gone the splint is removed, the ==cGMP gated ion channels close==, and the dark current is cut off.

### Hyperpolarisation in the light

Once the channels close, the ==Na⁺ inward current stops== while the ==K⁺ outward current of the inner segment is maintained==. Positive charge keeps leaving the cell but no longer enters, so the interior becomes more negative and the cell **hyperpolarises**: light produces the opposite of the depolarising receptor potential seen in most sensory receptors.

### Key numbers and names to memorise

Dark membrane potential ==≈ −40 mV==, against ≈ −70 mV in a typical neuron. Cascade order: light → **metarhodopsin II** → ==transducin== (the G protein) → ==cGMP phosphodiesterase== → cGMP falls → cGMP gated channels close → hyperpolarisation. Enzymes: **guanylyl cyclase** converts GTP to cGMP; **cGMP phosphodiesterase** converts cGMP to 5′GMP and ==is active in the light==.

### Summary

>Most neurons signal by depolarising: a stimulus drives the membrane toward threshold and an action potential follows. The photoreceptor works the other way round: it rests already depolarised in the dark, held near ==−40 mV== by the ==dark current==, and signals light by **hyperpolarising**, because the light cascade lowers cGMP, the splinted sodium channels close, and the unopposed K⁺ efflux makes the interior more negative. Same ionic logic as an ordinary neuron, with the sign reversed.
