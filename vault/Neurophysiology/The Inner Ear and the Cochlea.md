
>The **inner ear** is a ==fluid filled chamber== that receives the vibrations gathered by the middle ear and converts them into neural signals. Its auditory part is the **cochlea**, a coiled tube containing ==three fluid filled compartments== and the **Organ of Corti**, the sensory [[epithelium]] of hearing.

### From the Oval Window to the Cochlea

The inner ear is filled with fluid rather than air, so sound arriving from the middle ear must set that fluid in motion. The ==**oval window** transmits vibrations to the vestibule and on to the cochlea==, entering through the **scala vestibuli**. Because the fluid cannot be compressed, the pressure is relieved at the far end: the **scala tympani** ==is connected to the round window between the inner and middle ear==, which bulges outwards as the oval window is pushed in.

### The Three Chambers of the Cochlea

The figure shows one turn of the coiled cochlea in cross section, with the three chambers sitting in layers, one on top of the other, and ==their names must be known by heart==:

- **Scala vestibuli**, the upper chamber, filled with **perilymph**, continuous with the vestibule.
- **Scala media**, the middle chamber, filled with **endolymph**, ==unusually high in K+ concentration==.
- **Scala tympani**, the lower chamber, filled with **perilymph**, ending at the round window.

The names are easy to memorise because each chamber names its neighbour: the vestibuli continues the vestibule, the tympani faces the tympanic cavity of the middle ear, and the media lies in the middle. The fluids keep the same pattern of a pair and an exception: ==the two outer scalas share perilymph==, a fluid much like ordinary extracellular fluid and rich in Na+, while only the media holds endolymph, whose high K+ makes this chamber so unusual.

The **basilar membrane**, separates the scala media from the scala tympani. The second dividing wall, the **Reissner's membrane**, is a thin sheet that separates the scala media from the scala vestibuli above it. On the outer wall of the scala media sits the **stria vascularis**, the tissue that maintains the unusual ionic composition of the endolymph, and at the inner core lies the **spiral ganglion**, which houses the cell bodies of the auditory nerve fibres.

### The Organ of Corti

 The **Organ of Corti** is the sensory structure of the cochlea and rests on the ==basilar membrane== inside the scala media. It carries the **hair cells**: a single row of **inner hair cells** and, further out, three to four rows (five rows in some people) of **outer hair cells**. The two groups are held apart by the **tunnel of Corti**, an arch braced by the **rods of Corti**, stiff pillar shaped supporting cells that give the organ its rigidity, while the **basilar cells**, the remaining supporting cells, cradle the outer hair cells. From the apex of each hair cell protrude the **stereocilia**, the bundle of fine hair like processes that gives the cell its name and that reaches towards the **tectorial membrane**, the gelatinous sheet overlying the whole organ and resting against their tips. The top of the organ is capped by the **reticular lamina**, a stiff, continuous plate in which the flat apical ends of the hair cells fit together with the flattened tops of the supporting cells, the rods of Corti and the basilar cells, all locked by tight junctions into a single sealed sheet. The stereocilia poke out above this plate into the endolymph, while the cell bodies below it are bathed in perilymph like fluid, so the lamina keeps the two fluids apart and, because it is rigid, makes the whole organ move as one piece.

Two sets of nerve fibres serve the organ. The **afferent axons** of the neurons of the **spiral ganglion** contact the hair cells and gather to form the auditory nerve, which carries the signal to the brain, while the **efferent axons** arrive from the brainstem and bring modulatory input, mainly to the outer hair cells. How the bending of the stereocilia is converted into a receptor potential is covered in [[Hair Cells and Cochlear Transduction]].

### Summary

>The inner ear passes vibration from the oval window through the vestibule into the cochlea, a fluid filled tube of three chambers: the perilymph filled scala vestibuli and scala tympani flanking the endolymph filled scala media with its unusually high K+. The Organ of Corti rides on the basilar membrane within the scala media, its inner and outer hair cells reaching the tectorial membrane above and wiring into afferent and efferent axons below, while the round window relieves the pressure.
