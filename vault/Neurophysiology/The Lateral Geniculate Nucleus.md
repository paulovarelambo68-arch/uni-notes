
> The **lateral geniculate nucleus** (LGN) is the thalamic relay of the visual pathway: ==it receives 90% of retinal axons== and ==sends the signal on to the visual cortex==. It is organised into ==six layers of cell bodies== that keep the inputs of the two eyes and of the different ganglion cell types strictly segregated.

### Retinotopic organisation

The LGN holds a **retinotopic map**, meaning that neighbouring points on the retina project to neighbouring points in the nucleus, so the spatial order of the visual field is preserved at this relay. The map is heavily distorted in favour of central vision: ==50% of the representation is the fovea==, mirroring the fovea's dominance in visual acuity.

### The six layers

The LGN is built from ==six layers of cell bodies separated by intralaminar layers== (also called **koniocellular layers**, which ==take the [[P, M and K Ganglion Cells | K cell]] input==), and the layers are ==numbered 1 to 6, ventral to dorsal==. Each layer receives input from one eye only and from one ganglion cell type only, so the numbering tells you exactly which input sits where:

- Layers 1 and 2 are the ==magnocellular layers== and receive input from ==M cells==
- Layers 3 to 6 are ==the parvocellular layers== and receive input from ==P cells==
- Layers 1, 4 and 6 receive the ==contralateral nasal hemiretina==
- Layers 2, 3 and 5 receive the ==ipsilateral temporal hemiretina==

### Summary

> The lateral geniculate nucleus receives 90% of retinal axons, keeps a retinotopic map in which half of the representation is the fovea, and relays the signal to the visual cortex. Its six layers, numbered ventral to dorsal, are magnocellular (1 and 2, M cells) and parvocellular (3 to 6, P cells), with layers 1, 4 and 6 driven by the contralateral nasal hemiretina, layers 2, 3 and 5 by the ipsilateral temporal hemiretina, and K cells feeding the koniocellular layers in between.
