
> The **primary visual cortex** (**V1**) is ==visual area 1, Brodmann area 17==, the first cortical area to receive visual input. It is made up of ==six layers of cells==, takes its input from the [[The Lateral Geniculate Nucleus|lateral geniculate nucleus]] at layer 4, and assembles the whole visual scene from many small pieces of information, like a jigsaw.

### Layout of the six layers

The cortex is a sheet ==about 3 mm thick==, with the layers counted from layer 1 at the surface down to layer 6 just above the white matter. In the block figure the cells are also drawn grouped into vertical cylinders running through the whole sheet: each cylinder is a **column**, the stack of cells from all six layers at one spot. Both descriptions are true at once, and a cell's job is set by its address: ==its layer sets what it does in the circuit, its column sets which small piece of the scene it works on==.

Within the sheet, axons and dendrites run vertically between the layers to connect them, and each neuron keeps most of its dendrites close to its own cell body. Longer journeys use the white matter underneath, the myelinated cabling of the cortex: ==input arrives and output leaves through the white matter==, as the small arrows at the base show, with the LGN axons ascending from it to reach layer 4.

### The jigsaw idea

==The cortex assembles the whole visual scene from many small pieces of information, like a jigsaw==. No single piece of cortex deals with the scene as a whole; each small columnar unit handles only a tiny fragment of it. Coming back to the jigsaw example, on its own each piece carries almost nothing of the picture, and the complete image appears only when every piece is in place. In the same way, the perceived scene emerges only when the activity of all the small pieces is fitted together, which is exactly what the columnar organisation of V1 is built to do.

Assembly starts in V1 but is not finished there. V1 encodes the pieces and begins combining them within its columns, then passes the result on: ==V1 → V2 → V3 → MT== and ==V1 → V2 → V4== ([[Dorsal and Ventral Visual Pathways]]), and the later areas add their own specialisations, so the complete scene is put together progressively across the visual areas.

### Inputs from the lateral geniculate nucleus

==Inputs from the [[The Lateral Geniculate Nucleus | lateral geniculate nucleus]] enter in layer 4==, and ==layer 4 has four sublayers: 4A, 4B, 4Cα and 4Cβ==. The two main cell streams stay anatomically separate at this first stage: ==axons of M cells terminate on [[simple cells]] in 4Cα==, while ==axons of P cells terminate on simple cells in 4Cβ==. A third input runs alongside these two: ==axons from the intralaminar region of the LGN terminate in layers 2 and 3, where they innervate the blobs==.  

### Blobs

**Blobs** are ==small patches of cells in layers 2 and 3 of V1== that receive the axons from the ==intralaminar region of the [[The Lateral Geniculate Nucleus|LGN]]==. This input carries colour information, especially from short wavelength cones (S cones -> bluelight), so the blobs are the first cortical stage of this colour pathway.

In the figure they look exactly like their name. From the side, each blob is a small peg filling layers II and III, standing above the cells in layer IVA.

### Summary

> V1 is visual area 1, Brodmann area 17, a sheet about 3 mm thick with six layers, which receives the LGN input in layer 4 and works like a jigsaw, assembling the whole visual scene from many small pieces of information handled by separate columnar units. Within layer 4, M cell axons end on simple cells in 4Cα and P cell axons end on simple cells in 4Cβ, while axons from the intralaminar region of the LGN end in layers 2 and 3, where they innervate the blobs.
