
> ==Sleep unfolds in repeating cycles==: the brain descends from light sleep into deep slow wave sleep and back again, and each pass ends with a period of REM sleep. Across life this structure changes, with deep slow wave sleep giving way to lighter sleep and more frequent awakenings.

### The ninety minute cycle

After falling asleep, the brain descends through **N1** and **N2** into the **slow wave sleep** of **N3** and then returns towards light sleep; the full descent and return take ==about 90 minutes==. The ==first REM period begins about 90 minutes after falling asleep==, once this first descent and return is complete, and REM then recurs ==every 60 to 90 minutes==, each episode lasting ==about 20 minutes==. 

### The REM signature

REM sleep breaks the rule that deeper sleep means slower waves: its EEG is a ==faster, desynchronised rhythm of low amplitude==, remarkably similar to the waking record. That record is taken from the scalp and so reflects only the cortex; the hippocampus lies too deep to appear in it, and recordings from the hippocampus itself show ==[[EEG and the Stages of Sleep | theta]] activity at 4 to 8 Hz== during REM.

### Reading the traces and the hypnogram

The figure pairs the typical EEG trace of each stage with a ==hypnogram==, a graph that plots ==sleep stage against time across the whole night.== On the left, the **awake** record shows **alpha waves**, **N1** shows smaller, slower **theta waves**, **N2** is identified by a boxed **sleep spindle** and a **K complex**, **N3** shows the large, slow **delta waves** of deep sleep, and **REM** returns to a fast, low amplitude rhythm. On the right, the hypnogram runs over about **eight hours**: the trace steps down from wakefulness into N3, oscillates between depths, and marks each REM episode as an **orange bar**, ==five REM periods== in all, before the final awakening.

### Stage proportions in the young adult

The pie chart divides the young adult night into ==N1 about 5%, N2 about 45%, N3 about 25% and REM about 25%==: **N2** occupies the largest share, while **N1** is only a brief transition. In the hypnogram below, the night unfolds over ==about five cycles==: the descents into N3 are longest in the **first cycles**, whereas REM episodes ==come later in the night and last longer== each time. This young adult pattern is the reference, and ==a personal sleep score or tracker should be compared against it==.

### Sleep across life

The figure compares a **young child** with an **elderly adult** across the hours of the night. The child falls quickly into ==long, uninterrupted plateaus of N3== during the first hours and returns to REM at regular intervals, with almost no awakenings. The elderly adult spends ==little time in N3==, and the trace rises repeatedly to **Awake**, so the night breaks into fragments even though REM periods still appear. The rule is simple: ==less N3 and more awakenings as we age==.

### Summary

> A night of sleep is built from cycles of about 90 minutes, each running from N1 down to N3 and back before a REM episode of about 20 minutes, whose desynchronised EEG with hippocampal theta mimics wakefulness. Slow wave sleep concentrates in the early cycles while REM lengthens towards morning; with age, N3 shrinks and awakenings multiply.
