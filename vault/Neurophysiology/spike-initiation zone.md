
The axon hillock is the **trigger zone** because it has the **highest density of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels** in the neuron, meaning it has the _lowest threshold_ (~ −55 mV, versus a resting potential of about −70 mV).

Think of it as a **tally counter with a tripwire**:

1. EPSPs and IPSPs from all over the dendritic tree spread passively to the hillock (getting weaker with distance, which is why summation matters).
2. The hillock continuously "reads" the net membrane voltage.
3. **All-or-none rule:** if the summed potential reaches **threshold (−55 mV)**, the voltage-gated Na⁺ channels snap open, Na⁺ floods in, and a full-size **action potential** fires down the axon,  always at the same amplitude. If threshold isn't reached, **nothing happens**.

So it's not a dimmer switch, it's binary. The graded, analogue computation of the soma is converted into a digital (fire/don't fire) signal at the hillock. The _frequency_ of firing then encodes the strength of the stimulus (stronger input → threshold reached more often → higher firing rate → e.g., more muscle force via rate coding of motor units).
