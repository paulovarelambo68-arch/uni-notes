
A **synapse** is the specialised junction where one neuron communicates with another cell (neuron, muscle fibre, or gland). Key features:

- **Presynaptic terminal** (axon terminal of the sending neuron): contains vesicles full of neurotransmitter.
- **Synaptic cleft:** a tiny (~20–40 nm) gap between the cells.
- **Postsynaptic membrane** (of the receiving cell): studded with receptors.

**How it works:** an action potential reaches the terminal → voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open → Ca²⁺ influx triggers vesicles to fuse and release neurotransmitter into the cleft → neurotransmitter binds postsynaptic receptors → ion channels open → a voltage change (postsynaptic potential) is produced in the receiving cell.
